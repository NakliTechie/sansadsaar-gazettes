MINISTRY OF TEXTILES 
NOTIFICATION 
New Delhi, the 8th October, 2026 


G.S.R. 873(E).— Whereas the draft Textiles Committee (Adjudication of Penalties and Appeal) Rules, 2026 
were published, as required under sub-section (1) of section 22 of the Textiles Committee Act, 1963 (41 of 1963), vide 
notification of the Government of India, in the Ministry of Textiles, number G.S.R.746(E), dated the 18th August, 2026, 
published in the Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part I, Section 3, Sub-section (i), dated the 19th August, 2026, inviting 
suggestions or objections from all persons likely to be affected thereby, before the expiry of a period of thirty days from 
the date on which the copies of the Official Gazette containing the said notification were made available to the public; 


And whereas, copies of the said Official Gazette were made available to the public on the 19th August, 2026; 


And whereas, the said period of thirty days has expired and no objections or suggestions were received from 
the public on the said draft rules for consideration by the Central Government; 


Now, therefore, in exercise of the powers conferred by clauses (ea) and (eb) of sub-section (2) of section 22 read with 
sub-sections (1) and (2) of section 17A of the Textiles Committee Act, 1963 (41 of 1963), the Central Government 
hereby makes the following rules, namely:— 


1. Short title and commencement.— (1) These rules may be called the Textiles Committee (Adjudication and Manner 
of Holding Inquiry and Imposition of Penalty) Rules, 2026. 


(2) The rules shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette. 
2. Definitions.— (1) In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires,— 


(a) “Act” means the Textiles Committee Act, 1963 (41 of 1963); 
(b) “adjudicating officer” means the officer appointed as such under section 17A of the Act; 
(c) “appellate authority” means the officer appointed as such under sub-section (2) of section 17A of the Act; 


(d) “Collector” means the Collector of a district and includes any officer appointed or authorised by the State 
Government to discharge the functions of the Collector; 


(e) “Form” means a form appended to these rules. 


(f) “notice of demand” means a notice issued by the adjudicating officer in the prescribed Form-V requiring a person 
liable to pay any monetary penalty under the Act to pay the amount specified therein within the period specified 
in such notice; 


(2) The words and expressions used in these rules and not defined but defined in the Act, shall have the same meanings 
respectively assigned to them in the Act; 


3. Complaint.— (1) The Secretary, Textiles Committee or an officer of the Textiles Committee duly authorised by him 
in writing, on the basis of any report, inspection or reference received, may file a complaint in such format as specified 
in Form-I through electronic means or speed post with registration and proof of delivery or by hand to the adjudicating 
officer regarding any contravention committed under section 17 and section 18 of the Act. 


(2) Upon receipt of the complaint the adjudicating officer shall record the details of such complaint in the complaint 
register. 


4. Holding inquiry and imposing penalty.— (1) For the purpose of adjudication under sub-clauses (i) and (1) of clause 
(b) of sub-section (2) of section 17 of the Act and ascertaining whether any person has committed any contravention as 
specified in that section, the adjudicating officer shall issue a notice in Form-II to such person requiring him to show 
cause within such period as may be specified in such notice, being not less than seven days from the date of service 
thereof, as to why an inquiry should not be held against him.

(2) Every notice under sub-rule (1) shall indicate the nature of contravention alleged to have been committed. 


(3) The concerned person may submit his reply together with the supporting documents within a period of 
fifteen days from the date of receipt of the notice. 


(4) After considering the cause, if any, shown by such person, if the adjudicating officer is of the opinion that 
an inquiry should be held, he shall issue a notice requiring the appearance of that person personally or through a 
representative duly authorised by him on such date as may be fixed in the notice. 


(5) On the date fixed, the adjudicating officer shall explain to the person proceeded against or his authorised 
representative, the contravention committed by such person and the provision of the Act in respect of which 
contravention is alleged to have been committed. 


(6) The adjudicating officer shall, then, give an opportunity to such person to produce such documents or 
evidence as specified in Form-III as he may consider relevant to the inquiry and if necessary, the hearing may be 
adjourned to a future date and in taking such evidence the adjudicating officer shall not be bound to observe the 
provisions of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (47 of 2023). 


(7) While holding an inquiry under these rules, the adjudicating officer may require and enforce the attendance 
of any person acquainted with the facts and circumstances of the case to give evidence or to produce any document 
which in the opinion of the adjudicating officer may be useful for or relevant to the subject matter of the inquiry. 


(8) If any person fails, neglects or refuses to appear before the adjudicating officer as required under sub-rule 
(4), the adjudicating officer may proceed with the inquiry in the absence of such person after recording the reasons 
thereof. 


5. Order of adjudicating officer.— (1) If, upon consideration of the evidence produced before the adjudicating officer, 
the adjudicating officer is satisfied that the person has committed the contravention, he may, by order in writing, impose 
such penalty under the Act as he considers reasonable. 


(2) Every order made under sub-rule (1) shall specify the provision of the Act in respect of which 
contravention has been committed and shall contain the reasons for imposing the penalty. 


(3) A copy of the order made under this rule and all other copies of proceedings shall be supplied free of 
cost to the complainant and the person against whom the inquiry was held. 


(4) The adjudicating officer shall complete the proceedings within six months from the date of issuance of 
the notice to the opposite party. 


6. Factors to be considered while imposing penalty.— While determining the quantum of penalty under sub-section 
(2) of section 17 of the Act, the adjudicating officer may have due regard to the following factors, namely: — 


(a) the nature and gravity of the contravention; 

(b) whether the contravention is a first-time, repeated or continuing contravention; 
(c) the extent of loss or damage, if any, caused by the contravention; 

(d) any mitigating circumstances placed on record; and 


(e) any other factors as may be considered appropriate by the adjudicating officer. 


7. Copy of Order.— The adjudicating officer shall send a copy of every order made under these rules by him to the 
person on whom the penalty is imposed and to the Chairman of the Committee, Joint Secretary to the Government of 
India (ex-officio member of the Committee) and Secretary, Textiles Committee. 


8. Service of notices and orders.— (1) A notice or an order issued under these rules shall be served on the person 
against whom an inquiry is held, in any of the following manner, namely:— 


(i) by delivering or tendering it to such person or his authorised representative; or 


(ii) by sending it to such person through electronic means or by speed post with registration and proof of 
delivery to the address of his place of residence or his last known place of residence or the place where he 
carried on or last carried on business or personally works or last worked for gain; or 


(iii) if, it cannot be served in the manner specified under clause (i) or clause (ii), by affixing it on the outer door 
or some other conspicuous part of the premises in which that person resides or is known to have last resided or

Provided that, the appeal may be admitted after the expiry of the period of thirty days, if the appellant 
satisfies the appellate authority that he had sufficient cause for not preferring the appeal within such

The appeal shall be accompanied by a copy of order of the adjudicating officer issued under sub-rule (1)

The appeal shall be filed in triplicate by the appellant in person or by his duly authorised representative 
in writing duly appointed in this behalf or by speed post with registration and proof of delivery or through 
electronic means. 


The appeal sent by post shall be deemed to have been filed to the appellate authority on the day it is 
received. 


If on scrutiny, the appeal is found to be in order, it shall be admitted and if the appeal is found to be 
defective, the same shall, after notice to the party, be returned for compliance and if within a period of 
twenty-one days of receipt of such notice or within such extended time as may be granted, the defect is 
not rectified, the appellate authority, may, for reasons to be recorded in writing, decline to admit the 
appeal. 


A copy of the appeal shall be served by the appellate authority on the respondent, by hand or by speed 
post with registration and proof of delivery or through electronic means. 


The respondent may, within a period of thirty days of service of notice of appeal, file reply to the appellate 
authority. 


The appellate authority may call for the records relating to the proceedings from the respective 
adjudicating officer. 

The appellate authority may, after giving the parties to the appeal an opportunity of being heard, pass 
such orders as he may consider reasonable. 


The appellate authority shall dispose of the appeal within a period of sixty days from the date of filing of 
such appeal.

(5) The decision of the appellate authority shall be final and binding. 


11. Extension of time.— The adjudicating officer or the appellate authority may, for reasons to be recorded in writing, 
where there is a reasonable cause for the delay or failure to act, extend any period specified in these rules till such period 
as he considers reasonable. 


12. Order.— Every order under these rules, shall be dated, signed and communicated to all the parties. 


13. Rectification of the order.— The adjudicating officer or the appellate authority who has passed an order, may 
rectify any error apparent on the face of the record in such order, either on its own motion or where such error is brought 
to his notice by the affected person within a period of fifteen days from the date of such order. 


Explanation: For the purpose of this rule, “error apparent on the face of record” shall mean any typographical 
errors that creep in inadvertently into the order and includes such other errors that do not require a long drawn out 
reasoning process to ascertain such a mistake. 


14. Manner of recovery of penalty.— (1) Where a penalty is imposed by the adjudicating officer under the Act, and 
in the event of filing of an appeal such an order is confirmed by the appellate authority, and such penalty is not deposited 
within the period of sixty days from the date of passing the order, or the order was confirmed in appeal, as the case may 
be, the adjudicating officer shall issue a notice of demand in such format as specified in Form-V to the person liable to 
pay the penalty. 


(2) If the amount is not paid within the period specified in the notice of demand under sub-rule (1), the adjudicating 
officer shall forward a recovery certificate to the Collector of the district in which the defaulter resides, or carries on 
business, or works for gain, or owns property. 


(3) On receipt of the certificate under sub-rule (2), the Collector shall proceed to recover the amount stated in 
it, together with the cost of recovery, as an arrear of land revenue, in accordance with the Revenue Recovery Act, 1890 
(1 of 1890), or other applicable laws for the time being in force.
