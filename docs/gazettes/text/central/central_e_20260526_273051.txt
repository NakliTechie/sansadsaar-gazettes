WHEREAS, the Sohelwa or Soheldeo Wildlife Sanctuary is located in Balrampur and Shravasti districts of 
Uttar Pradesh State. The Sanctuary lies close to the Indo-Nepal border and is spread over an area of 452.62 square 
kilometres. The Wildlife Sanctuary is located between 27° 55' 42.07" N, 081° 58' 13.47" E to 27° 40' 48.09" N, 082° 
28' 57.21" E and 27° 52' 19.50" N, 081° 55' 39.38" E to 27° 35' 49.56" N, 082° 30' 21.82" E; 


AND WHEREAS the wildlife Sanctuary includes very rich & dense forest and biodiversity. It is an 
Integral part of Tarai-Arc-landscape. On the basis of Bio-Geographical classification, Sohelwa Wildlife 
Sanctuary fall under the 7A-Upper Gangetic Plains Tarai Bhabar region. “Tharu” tribal species which are 
Mangol look-a-like residing in some areas of the sanctuary are the uniqueness of the sanctuary. The Sanctuary 
represents the moist sloping narrow Bhabhar belt in the southern foothills of the Churiya Hills (Shivalik 
extension of the outer Himalayas in Nepal), which is formed by the deposition of rounded gravel, pebbles and 
boulders brought by rivers and streams; 


AND WHEREAS the sanctuary serves as an important stopover point for the population of tigers 
between the eastern border region Chitwan-Valmiki-Parsa Tiger Conservation Unit and the north-western 
Bardia-Banke Tiger Conservation Unit with low tiger habitat, which helps in facilitating uninterrupted genetic 
exchange within the Terai-Arc landscape. It is a continuous forest area surrounded by the Churiya mountain 
range of Nepal nation and connected to Banke National Park of Nepal by Kamdi corridor. Elephants also keep 
coming here through Kamdi corridor; 


AND WHEREAS the sanctuary harbors scattered tropical dry deciduous type of forests, which are found in a 
large area of the extreme western and most eastern areas towards the north of the Sal belt, where Anogeissus latifolia, 
Terminalia ellipticam and Adina cordifolia are the main species. The forest also has the natural presence of endangered 
plant species such as Hathi Paula/Gainti (Indopiptadenia oudhensis) and Saksal (Darbergia latifolia). In the new lower 
alluvial Bhabhar areas among Sal and other scattered forests, typical sequential forests of pioneer species such as 
Sheesham (Dalbergia sissoo), Khair (Acacia catechu) and Dhamina (Holoptelea integrifolia). Downwards in the Terai 
Formation, where the soil changes from sandy and boulder-rich to clayey, Jamun (Syzygium cumini) is found along the 
banks of the rivulets. The other tree species found in the area are Shorea robusta, Terminalia genus, Terminalia elliptica, 
Terminalia arjuna, Bauhinia variegata, Terminalia chebula, Terminalia bellirica, Schleichera oleosa, Albizia lebbeck, 
Adina cordifolia, Trewia nudiflora, Acacia catechu, Ficus racemosa, Toona ciliata, Pterocarpus marsupium, Aegle 
marmelos, Madhuca longifolia, Lannea coromandelica, Butea monosperma, Stereospermum chelonoides, Mallotus 
Philippensis, Tectona grandis, Bombax ceiba etc.; 


AND WHEREAS different types of mammals are found in the sanctuary such as Leopard, Bear, Wolf, Hyena, 
Jackal, Wild Boar, Sambhar, Spotted Deer, Neelgai, Barking Deer along with Monkeys of different types. In birds little 
Grebe, Indian Cormorant, Little Cormorant, Darter, Indian Pond Heron, Cattle-egret, Great-egret, little egret, Common 
Teal, Spot-Billed Duck, Black-Shouldered Kite, Black Kite, Greater Spotted Eagle, Long-Billed Vulture, White 
Rumped Vulture, Grey Francolin, Sarus Crane, Water Cock, Common Moorhen, Purple Moorhen, Pheasant-Tailed 
Jacana, Bronze-Winged Jacana. In migratory birds Painted Star, Asian Openbill, Black-headed Ibis, Eurasian Spoonbill, 
Grey leg goose, Bar-Headed Goose etc.; 


AND WHEREAS it is necessary to conserve and protect the area, extent and boundary which is specified in 
paragraph 1 of this notification around the protected area of Sohelwa Wildlife Sanctuary as Eco-sensitive zone from 
ecological, environmental and biodiversity point of view and to prohibit industries or class of industries and their 
operations and processes in the said Eco-Sensitive Zone;
