G.S.R. 909(E).— In exercise of the powers conferred by, sub-section () and clauses 
(a), (b), (g) and (h) of sub-section (2) of section 30 of the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 
(24 of 2025), and in supersession of the Merchant Shipping (Ships and Port Facility 
Security) Rules, 2024, except as respects things done or omitted to be done before such 
supersession, the Central Government hereby makes the following rules, namely:— 


i. Short title and commencement. — (i) These rules may be called the Merchant 
Shipping (Ships and Port Facility Security) Rules, 2026. 


(2) They shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official 
Gazette. 


2. Application. — Unless otherwise expressly provided, these rules shall apply to the 
matters relating to safety and security of — 


(a) every Indian ship including mobile offshore drilling units and high-speed craft as 
defined in regulation XI-2/ of the Safety Convention, irrespective of its type or area of 
operation, wherever it may be, which meets with the requirements specified in 
Schedule |: 


(b) any ship other than an Indian ship, when such ship calls at any Indian port or Indian 
offshore facility, or is at anchorage within Indian jurisdiction; 


(c) any Indian port facility; and 


(d) any company owning, operating, or managing any vessel or port facility to which 
these rules apply.

ship-related security activities comply with the applicable requirements of the 
Safety Convention, considering the approved security plan; 


(d) “company” means the company as defined under clause (a) of section 5 
of the Act; 


(e) “company security officer’ means the person designated by a company under sub- 
rule ({) of rule 4;

4. Responsibility with respect to security. — (i) The designated authority shall set the 
appropriate security level for every port facility within its jurisdiction and shall ensure that 
information on the applicable security level, and any change therein, is communicated 
without delay to the port facilities concerned and to ships before entering, or while within, 
any port within its jurisdiction. 


(2) The Administration shall set the appropriate security levels for ships entitled to fly its flag 
and ensure timely communication of such information on such security level and any change 
in the security level shall be updated as per the circumstances. 


(3) In determining the security level referred to in sub-rules () and (2), due regard shall be 
given to— 


(a) the credibility of threat information; 
(b) the degree of corroboration of such information; 
(c) the specificity or imminence of the threat; and 
(d) the potential consequences of a security incident. 
(4) Subject to regulations XI-2/3 and XI-2/7 of the Safety Convention, the Administration 


and the designated authority, as the case may be, shall provide guidance for protection 
against security incidents. 


(5) The designated authority may authorise a recognised security organisation to undertake 
security related duties specified under Chapter XI-2 of the Safety Convention and Part A of 
the ISPS Code, as it considers appropriate, except the following, namely: —

security assessments and port facility security plans; 


(0) approving a port facility security assessment and a port facility security plan, 
and amendments thereto; 


(c) determining the port facilities required to designate a port facility security 
officer; 


(6) The Administration shall be responsible for — 


(a) ensuring protection of security-sensitive material relating to ship security 
assessments and ship security plans; 


(0) exercising control and compliance measures in respect of ships in

(c) establishing the requirements for a declaration of security as referred to in

mitigation measures to the International Maritime Organization and making 
such information available to the public; and 


(e) resolving any inconsistency between safety and security requirements in 
ship operations to ensure that safety requirements prevail, and issuing 
necessary directions. 


(7) The Administration and the designated authority shall, to the extent considered 
appropriate, test the effectiveness of ship security plans and port facility security plans, 
including amendments thereto. 


5. Power of master for ship safety and security. — (!) The master shall not be 
constrained by the company, the charterer, or any other person from taking or executing 
any decision which, in the professional judgement of the master, is necessary for 
maintaining the safety and security of the ship including the authority to deny access to any 
person (other than those duly authorised by the Administration) or their effects and to refuse 
loading of cargo, containers or other closed cargo transport units. 


(2) Where a conflict arises between the safety and security of the ship during its operations, 
the master— 


(a) shall give effect to those requirements necessary to maintain the safety of the 
ship; 


(6) may implement temporary security measures; 


(c) shall inform the Administration or the contracting government in whose port the 
ship is operating or intending to enter. 


(3) Any temporary security measures adopted under sub-rule (2) shall, to the highest 
practicable degree, be proportionate with the prevailing security level.

6. Declaration of security. — (7) The Administration or the designated authority, as the 
case may be, shall determine as to when a declaration of security is required having regard 
to the risk posed by the_ship and port interface or ship-to-ship activity, to persons, property, 
or the environment. 


(2) A ship may request a declaration of security in the following circumstances, namely:— 


(a) the ship is operating at a higher security level than the port facility, or another 
ship is interfacing with it; or 


(0) there is an agreement on declaration of security between contracting 
governments covering certain international voyages or specific ships on those 
voyages; or 


(c) there has been a security threat or a security incident involving the ship or port 
facility; or 


(d) the ship is at a port which is not required to have an approved port facility security 
plan or its implementation; or 


(e) the ship is conducting ship-to-ship activities with another ship not required to 
have an approved ship security plan or its implementation. 


(3) Any request for a declaration of security shall be acknowledged by the applicable port 
facility or ship in accordance with Schedule-lll in English. 


(4) The declaration of security shall be completed by the — 
(a) master or the ship security officer on behalf of the ship; and 


(0) port facility security officer or, if the designated authority determines otherwise, 
by any other body responsible for shore-side security, on behalf of the port facility. 


(5) The declaration of security shall address the security requirements that may be shared 
between a port facility and a ship or between the ships and shall state the responsibility for 
each of them. 


(6) Every ship shall keep records of the declaration of security for a minimum period of three 
years. 


(7) A change in the security level may require a new or revised declaration of security.

(2) The ship security alert system, when activated, shall — 


(a) initiate and transmit a ship-to-shore security alert to the concerned company and 
the centre designated by the Administration, which shall identify the ship, its location 
and indicate whether the security of the ship is under threat or it has been 
compromised; 


(0) not send the ship security alert to any other ship; 

(c) not raise any alarm on board the ship; and 

(d) continue the ship security alert until deactivated or reset. 
(3) The ship security alert system shall — 


(a) be capable of being activated from the navigation bridge and in at least one other

(5) When an alert of a security situation is received from an Indian registered vessel, the

(6) On receipt of the information of ship security alert from an Indian ship, the centre
