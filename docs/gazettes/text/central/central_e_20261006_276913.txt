DRAFT RULES 
1. Short title and commencement. — (1) These rules may be called the Prevention of 
Cruelty to Animals (Control and Supervision of Experiment, Breeding and Trading of 
Laboratory Animals) Rules, 2026. 


(2) They shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette. 
(3) These Rules will establish measures for the protection of animals used for scientific or 
educational purposes. 


(4) To that end, these rules lays down guidelines for the following: 


(a) the replacement and reduction of the use of animals in experiments and the refinement of 
the breeding, accommodation, care and use of animals in experiments; 

(b) the origin, breeding, marking, care and accommodation and euthanasia of animals; 

(c) the operation of breeders, traders and users;

(9) the evaluation and authorisation of projects involving the use of animals in experiments 


(5) These Rules shall apply where animals are used or intended to be used in experiments or 
bred specifically so that their organs or tissues may be used for scientific purposes. 


(6) These Rules shall apply until the animals have been euthanized, rehabilitated to a suitable 
habitat or husbandry system upon completion of experimentation. 


(7) The elimination of pain, suffering, distress or lasting harm by the successful use of anaesthesia, 
analgesia or other methods shall not exclude the use of an animal in experiments from the scope 
of these Rules. 


(8) These Rules shall apply to live non-human vertebrate animals, including independent feeding 
larval forms of fishes, embryonated eggs and foetal forms of mammals as from the last third stage 
of their normal development; 


(9) These Rules shall apply to animals used in experiments, which are at an earlier stage of 
development than that referred to sub-rule 8, if the animal is to be allowed to live beyond that stage 
of development and, as a result of the experiments performed, is likely to experience pain, 
suffering, distress or lasting harm after it has reached that stage of development. 


(10) These Rules shall not apply to the following: 


(a) non-experimental agricultural practices; 

(b) non-experimental clinical veterinary practices; 

(c) veterinary clinical field trial required for the marketing authorization of a legally 
approved veterinary medicinal product except test batch and quality control trial for 
batch manufacturing; 

(d) practices undertaken for the purposes of recognised animal husbandry; 

(e) practices undertaken for the primary purpose of identification of an animal; 

(f) practices not likely to cause pain, suffering, distress or lasting harm equivalent to, or 
higher than, that caused by the introduction of a needle in accordance with good 
veterinary practice.

3. Replacement, Reduction and Refinement (3Rs). - For the purpose of implementation of

out the experiment shall ensure.-

project;

(3) for any of the aims in sub-rule (2) in the development, manufacture or testing of the quality,

socially aware nominee to carry out periodical inspections not less than two times in a years, apart 
from the meeting, to oversee the animal house with regard to their fulfilment of the CCSEA 
guidelines, welfare of animals housed in the establishment; 


(2) The main nominee shall submit such inspection report as per Schedule VIII format annexed to 
these Rules; 


(3) Any violations of the CCSEA rules, guidelines and prescribed standards observed by the main 
nominee and socially aware nominee shall be reflected in the Minutes of the IAEC meeting and 
also reported to the CCSEA;

with prior intimation and such report shall be submitted as per Schedule IT format.

received from the main nominee or socially aware nominee in accordance with Rule 24 or any 
complaints made by any person, organization which have been verified through inspections, if

committee shall.- 


(a) issue a deficiency notice specifying the nature of non-compliance and requiring corrective 
action within 15 days of issuance of notice; 


(b) where the establishment fails to comply with the deficiency noticed within 15 days or where 
repeated instances of violation occurred within six months, the Committee may, and after 
giving the establishment a reasonable opportunity of being heard, suspend or revoke the 
registration.

Classification of Severity of Experiments 


The severity of an experiment shall be determined by the degree of pain, suffering, distress or 
lasting harm expected to be experienced by an individual animal during the course of experiment. 


Section I: Severity categories.- 

1. Non-recovery: 
Experiments which are performed entirely under general anaesthesia from which the animal 
shall not recover consciousness shall be classified as ‘non-recovery’. 


2. Mild: 
Experiments on animals that are likely to experience short-term mild pain, suffering or 
distress, as well as experiments with no significant impairment of the well-being or general 
condition of the animals shall be classified as ‘mild’. 


3. Moderate: 
Experiments on animals as a result of which the animals are likely to experience short-term 
moderate pain, suffering or distress, or long-lasting mild pain, suffering or distress as well 
as experiments that are likely to cause moderate impairment of the well-being or general 
condition of the animals shall be classified as ‘moderate’. 


4. Severe: 
Experiments on animals as a result of which the animals are likely to experience severe pain, 
suffering or distress, or long-lasting moderate pain, suffering or distress as well as 
experiments, that are likely to cause severe impairment of the well-being, or general condition 
of the animals shall be classified as ‘severe’. 


Section II: Assignment criteria.- 

The assignment of the severity category shall consider any intervention or manipulation of an 
animal within a defined experiment. It shall be based on the most severe effects likely to be 
experienced by an individual animal after applying all appropriate refinement techniques. 


When assigning an experiment to a particular category, the type of experiment and several other 
factors should be considered. All these factors should be considered on a case-by-case basis. 


The factors related to the experiment shall include: 

(i) type of manipulation, handling, 

(ii) nature of pain, suffering, distress or lasting harm caused by (all elements of) the experiment, 
and its intensity, the duration, frequency and multiplicity of techniques employed, 


(iii) Cumulative suffering within an experiment,
