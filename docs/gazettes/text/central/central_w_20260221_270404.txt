2I. It is a settled principle of law that a decision by the Hon’ble High Court in Writ Petitions under Article 226 
are subjected to res judicata and binding the parties in later litigation reinforcing Section |l of the Code of Civil 
Procedure. In essense, a binding decision of Hon’ble High Court brings closure to that specific dispute fostering 
judicial efficiency and preventing endless litigation. 


22. The Hon’ble Apex Court in Pooja Ferro Alloys Pvt. Ltd vs. State of Goa c/w others (2025 INSC 27) 
concurrently quoted the following principle from Satyadhyan Ghosal vs. Deorajin Debi (960 3 SCR 590) 


“The principle of res judicata is based on the need of giving a finality to judicial decisions. What it says 
is that once a res is judicata, it shall not be adjudged again. Primarily it applies as between past 
litigation and future litigation. When a matter — whether on a question of fact or a question of law — 
has been decided between two parties in one suit or proceeding and the decision is final, either because 
no appeal was taken to a higher court or because the appeal was dismissed, or no appeal lies, neither 
party will be allowed in a future suit or proceeding between the same parties to canvass the matter 
again. This principle of res judicata is embodied in relation to suits in Section I of the Code of Civil 
Procedure; but even where Section does not apply, the principle of res judicata has been applied by 
courts for the purpose of achieving finality in litigation. The result of this is that the original court as 
well as any higher court must in any future litigation proceed on the basis that the previous decision was 
correct. 


The principle of res judicata applies also as between two stages in the same litigation to this extent that a 
court, whether the trial court or a higher court having at an earlier stage decided a matter in one way 
will not allow the parties to re-agitate the matter again at a subsequent stage of the same proceedings.

Accordingly heard learned counsel for respondent and gone through the records. 

In view of the above said facts the claimant/workman has not field any rejoinder/evidence in support of his case on 


affidavit, in spite of several opportunities given to him and taking into consideration the law as laid by the Hon'ble 
High Court in the case of V. K. Raj Industries v. Labour Court (7) and others 7967 (29) FLR 94 as under: 


“It is well settled that if a party challenges the legality of an order, the burden lies upon him to prove 
illegality of the order and if no evidence is produced the party invoking jurisdiction of the Court must fail. 
Whenever a workman raises a dispute challenging the validity of the termination of service if is imperative 
for him to file written statement before the Industrial Court setting out grounds on which the order is 
challenged and he must also produce evidence to prove his case. If the workman fails to appear or to file 
written statement or produce evidence, the dispute referred by the State Government cannot be answered in 
favour of the workman and he would not be entitled to any relief.” 

In the case of M/s Uptron Powertronics Employees’ Union, Ghaziabad through its Secretary v. Presiding Officer, 

Labour Court (II), Ghaziabad and others 2008 (776) FLR 7764 Hon’ble Allahabad High Court has held as under:

FLR 38 and Meritech India Ltd. v. State of U.P. and Ors. 7996 FLR that in the absence of any evidence led 
by or on behalf of the workman the reference is bound to be answered by the court against the workman. In 
such a situation it is not necessary for the employers to lead any evidence at all. The obligation to lead 
evidence to establish an allegation made by a party is on the party making the allegation. The test would be, 
who would fail if no evidence is led.”’ 

And by the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in the case of District Administrative Committee, U.P. P.A.C.C.S.C. 

Services v. Secretary-cum-G.M. District Co-operative Bank Ltd. 2070 (726) FLR 529; wherein it has been held as 

under: 
“The submission is that even if the petitioner failed to lead the evidence, burden was on the shoulders of the

By order No. K-0/l-7/2020-IR dated 8.0!.202l the present industrial dispute has been referred for 
adjudication to this Tribunal in exercise of the powers conferred by clause (d) of sub-section () and sub-section (2A) 
of Section l0 the Industrial Disputes Act, 947 (4 of 947) by the Central Government, with following schedule: 


“Whether Shri Sanfrrp Kumar Gupta, Bss Flm Engineer can be treated as workman? If so, whether the 
alleged termination of his services by the management of Nokia Solutions and Networks India Pvt. Ltd./ 
Vodafone Idea Limited is legal & justified? If not, to what relief the concerned workman is entitled to and 
from which date?" 


Accordingly, an industrial dispute No. 0/202] has been registered on 28.0.202]. 
On 06.08.202! claimant filed claim statement supported by an affidavit. 


Facts stated in the claim petition are in brief that claimant was initially appointed with the respondent; however 
without following the provision of retrenchment as provided under section 25 (F), 25 (G) & 25 (H) of the Industrial 
Dispute Act !947 (hereinafter referred to as the Act) his services were dispensed on 2.0.2020. 


On behalf of the respondent no. 2 statement of defense filed on !2.03.2024 and respondent no. | filed its written 
statement on |.0.2023 in which preliminary objection also taken by the respondent. 


After filing of the written statement by respondent, in spite of opportunities given to workman, he neither filed 
rejoinder nor evidence in support of his case on affidavit 


Accordingly heard learned counsel for respondent and gone through the records. 


In view of the above said facts the claimant/workman has not field any rejoinder/evidence in support of his case on 
affidavit, in spite of several opportunities given to him and taking into consideration the law as laid by the Hon'ble 
High Court in the case of V. K. Raj Industries v. Labour Court (7) and others 7967 (29) FLR 94 as under: 


“It is well settled that if a party challenges the legality of an order, the burden lies upon him to prove 
illegality of the order and if no evidence is produced the party invoking jurisdiction of the Court must fail. 
Whenever a workman raises a dispute challenging the validity of the termination of service if is imperative 
for him to file written statement before the Industrial Court setting out grounds on which the order is 
challenged and he must also produce evidence to prove his case. If the workman fails to appear or to file 
written statement or produce evidence, the dispute referred by the State Government cannot be answered in 
favour of the workman and he would not be entitled to any relief.” 


In the case of M/s Uptron Powertronics Employees’ Union, Ghaziabad through its Secretary v. Presiding Officer, 
Labour Court (II), Ghaziabad and others 2008 (7768) FLR 7764 Hon’ble Allahabad High Court has held as under: 


“The law has been settled by the Apex Court in case of Shanker Chakravarti v. Britannia Biscuit Co. Ltd., 
V.K. Raj Industries v. Labour Court and Ors., Airtech Private Limited v. State of U.P. and Ors. /964 (49) 
FLR 38 and Meritech India Ltd. v. State of U.P. and Ors. 7996 FLR that in the absence of any evidence led 
by or on behalf of the workman the reference is bound to be answered by the court against the workman. In 
such a situation it is not necessary for the employers to lead any evidence at all. The obligation to lead 
evidence to establish an allegation made by a party is on the party making the allegation. The test would be, 
who would fail if no evidence is led.” 


And by the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in the case of District Administrative Committee, U.P. P.A.C.C.S.C. 
Services v. Secretary-cum-G.M. District Co-operative Bank Ltd. 2070 (726) FLR 59; wherein it has been held as 
under: 


“The submission is that even if the petitioner failed to lead the evidence, burden was on the shoulders of the 
respondent to prove the termination order as illegal. He was required to lead evidence first which he failed. 
A perusal of the impugned award also does not show that any evidence either oral or documentary was led 
by the respondent. In the case of no evidence, the reference has to be dismissed.” 


As the workman has not filed any rejoinder/affidavit (examination-in-chief) so it is a case of no evidence filed on 
behalf of workman in support of case set up by her, liable to be dismissed. 


For the foregoing reasons, the case is dismissed and; and the workman is not entitled for any relief.

Findings 
Issue No. I:- 


In this context, Respondent has submitted that the Respondent, Export Inspection Agency, Chennai, is a 
regulatory authority operating under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry in the field of pre-shipment inspection of 
notified commodities, and it was set up by the Central Government under Section 7 of the export, (quality control, and 
inspection Act, 963). Therefore, the claim statement is not maintainable as the Respondent is a Central Government 
department, statutory body under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, Government of India. And as such, this 
Tribunal has no jurisdiction to try this dispute under the provision of Industrial Dispute Act, |947. 


In this context, Petitioner, in his chief statement affidavit, has nowhere stated that contention of Respondent 
in the counter, that the Export Inspection Agency of Chennai is a regulatory and operational body, and it does not 
come under the definition of "industry" as provided under the Industrial Disputes Act, is incorrect: undisputedly 
Respondent is governed by the Export, (quality control, inspection Act, 963). And its main function is to ensure 
quality control and pre-shipment inspection for goods being exported from India. Therefore, the Respondent Export 
Inspection Council of India, is not covered under the definition of "industry" as provided under Section 2F of the 
Industrial Disputes Act. The Export Inspection Counsel operate under the separate Act which focus on advising the 
Government and ensure quality control for Export rather than its activities like inspection and certification are part of 
its function as a statutory body for trade not industrial activity as defined under I.D Act !947. industrial Disputes 
Therefore, the claim statement of the petitioner raising industrial dispute is not maintainable under the provision of 
Industrial Dispute Act. This issue is decided against the petitioner. 


Issue No.II:- 


In this context, petitioner, in his claim statement, has alleged that he joined the service of the Respondent on 
March 2002, at the Bhimavaram office, and worked till 4" July 2008, and his service has been terminated by reports 
without assigning any reason in gross violation of the provision contained under Section 25F and 25N of the 
Industrial Disputes Act. Petitioner has alleged that has termination order dated 57 July 2008 is illegal, liable to be set 
aside. In support of his allegation. Petitioner has filed the affidavit of chief statement of his witness ४१७], Therein 
१४५४] has reiterated the averment made in the claim statement. Whereas, in the cross-examination, WW has stated 
that the Assistant director of Respondent management, Shri G. V. George, engaged him on duty and there was no 
notification published for the post in any newspaper. Further, WW] states that on the verbal request of the Assistant 
director, he joined the work. Further, witness states that there was no designation for his post and no appointment 
letter was issued to him. His name was not sponsored through the employment exchange. Further, the WW state that 
he felt there was vacancy for four posts, and he may be absorbed in one post and he doesn't know whether the four 
vacant posts were sanctioned or not. Further, WWI witness states that he has made four representations to the 
Chennai head office and also to the office bearer of the Respondent office in the year 200I, and he was paid Rs. 20/- 
per day and later on he was paid Rs. 25/-, and Rs. 35/-, and lastly, he was getting Rs. 70/- per day. Further, the 
witness states that it is not correct to say that the amount of Rs. 20/- was paid to him in the year 200! for hourly basis. 
The witness WWI admitted that it is not an industry, but it is a central government agency, and there was no 
manufacturing or production in the Respondent office. Thus, in view of the statement made by the witness WWI in 
his cross-examination, it manifests that the petitioner had worked as a daily wager in the Respondent office and he 
was not issued any appointment letter for the post. The document filed by the petitioner in support of his allegation do 
not support the averment of the petitioner that he was appointed in the Respondent office on Group D post. However, 
petitioner has not filed any appointment letter, Attendance Register or salary slip in support of his averment that he 
was appointed by the Respondent. The document exhibit W3, is a letter dated 267 December 2008, issued by the 
Export Inspection Agency Chennai in respect of the representation of petitioner, A. Mallikarjuna Rao, it is mentioned 
therein that the petitioner A. Mallikarjuna Rao had worked intermittently for a few hours a day on a daily basis, as 
and when his services were required. Further, it is mentioned that no order of appointment was issued to A. 
Mallikarjuna Rao at any point of time. 


As regard the contention of the petitioner that he has been terminated by the Respondent from the 
employment in gross violation of section 25F and 25N of the ID Act, Before examining the legality of termination of 
Petitioner it would be apposite to mention the relevant provision contained under section 25F of the IT Act, as 
extracted hereunder:-

25-F. Conditions precedent to retrenchment of workmen:- No workman employed in any industry who has been 
in continuous service for not less than one year under an employer shall be retrenched by that employer until- 


(a) the workman has been given one month's notice in writing indicating the reasons for retrenchment and the 
period of notice has expired, or the workman has been paid in lieu of such notice, wages for the period of the 
notice. 


[xxx] 


(b) the workman has been paid, at the time of retrenchment, compensation which shall be equivalent to fifteen days' 
average pay [for every completed year of continuous service] or any part thereof in excess of six months; and 


(c) notice in the prescribed manner is served on the appropriate Government [or such authority as may be specified by 
the appropriate Government by notification in the Official Gazette]. 


Further, in this context, Hon’ble Court has laid down principles in respect of continuous service under the 
provision contained under section 25F of the ID Act 947 and also burden of proof these decision of Hon’ble Court 
are being discussed as here under:- 


(a).Further, in Rajasthan State Ganganagar S. Mills Ltd. v. State of Rajasthan and Anr. (2004) Apex Court held: 


"It was the case of the workman that he had worked for more than 240 days in the year concerned. This 
claim was denied by the appellant. It was for the claimant to lead evidence to show that he had worked for 240 days in 
the year preceding the date of his termination. He has filed an affidavit. It is statement which is in his favor and that 
cannot be regarded as sufficient evidence for any Court or Tribunal to come to the conclusion that in fact the claimant 
had worked for 240 days in a year." 


(b).In the case of Mohan Lal v. Management, BEL 498] SCC P. 225. Hon'ble Apex Court held:- 


“Before a workman can claim retrenchment, not being in consonance of Section 25 of the ID act he has to 
show that he has been in continuous service of not less than | year with the employer who had retrenched him from 
service The Hon'ble Apex Court in this case also laid down the principle of how to count 240 days of service within 
one year and held: "Clause (2)(a) provides for a fiction to treat a workman in continuous service for a period of one 
year despite the fact that he has not rendered uninterrupted service for a period of one year but he has rendered service 
period of 240 days during the period of !2 calendar months for counting backwards and just preceding the relevant 
date being the date of retrenchment. In other words, in order to invoke the fiction enacted in clause (2)(a) it is 
necessary to determine first the relevant date, ie the date of termination of service which is complained of as 
retrenchment. After that date is ascertained move backward to a period of !2 months just preceding the date of 
retrenchment and then ascertain whether within the period of 2 months, the workman has rendered service for a 
period of 240 days. If these three facts are affirmatively answered in favor of the workman pursuant to the deeming 
fiction enacted in clause (2)(a) it will have to be assumed that the workman is in continuous service for a period of 
one year and he will satisfy the eligibility qualification enacted in Section 25-F”’ 


(c).In the case of GM., BSNL and others V. Mahesh Chand AIR 2008 SC (Supp) 328, wherein the Hon'ble Apex 
Court have held, 


"It was the case of the claimant that he had so worked but this claim was denied by the appellant. It was then 
for the claimant to lead evidence to show that he had in fact worked for 240 days in the year preceding his 
termination. Filing of an affidavit is only his own statement in his favour and that cannot be regarded as 
sufficient evidence forany Court or Tribunal to come to the conclusion that a workman had, in fact, worked for 240 
days in a year. No proof of receipt of salary or wages for 240 days or order or record of appointment or engagement 
for this period was produced by the workman. On this ground alone, the award is liable to be set aside." 


(d). In the case of Range Forest Officer Vs. S T Hadimani AIR 2002 SC page !47, wherein Hon'ble Apex Court 
have held, 


"the onus lies upon claimant to show that he had in fact worked for 240 days in a year-In absence of proof of 
receipt of salary or wages record of appointment, filing of an affidavit by workman is not sufficient evidence to prove 
that he had worked for 240 days in a year preceding his termination.” 


(e). In the case of Essen Deinki Vs. Rajiv Kumar, AIR 2003 SC 38 the Hon'ble Apex Court have held, 


"It was for the employee concerned to prove that he has in fact completed 240 days in the last preceding 2 
months’ period." 


(f). In the case of Municipal Corporation, Faridabad Vs. Siri Niwas AIR 2004 SC 468, wherein Hon'ble Apex Court 
have held:- 


"that the burden of proof was on the workman to show that he had worked for 240 days in the preceding 2 
months prior to his alleged retrenchment u/s 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, !947. It is also held that it is 


[भाग ]--खण्ड 3(ii)] भारत का राजपत्र : फरवरी 2, 2026/फाल्गुन 2, 947 85 


improbable that a person working in a Local Authority would not be in possession of any documentary evidence to 
support his claim before the tr. Apart from muster rolls he could have shown the terms and conditions of his offer of 
appointment and the remuneration received by him for working during the afore mentioned period. He even did not 
examine any other witness in support of his case." 


(g). In the case of Chief Engineer, Ranjit Sagar Dam and another Vs. Sham Lal, 2006 AIR SCW 3574, wherein the 
Hon'ble Apex Court have held: 


"Fact that workman had worked for 240 days or more in year immediately preceding termination Burden of proof 
Burden is on workman not on employer-delay of nine years in seeking-no relief could be given to claimant." 
Manager, RBI, Bangalore vs. S. Mani & Ors., on 4.3.2005 AIR 2005 Supreme Court 2!79 have held that, merely the 
completion of 240 days of continuous service by the workmen by itself does not give rise to claim of permanence in 
the employment Hon'ble Supreme Court have held that:- 


"It may not be out of place to mention that completion of 240 days of continuous service in a year may not 
by itself be a ground for directing an order of regularization. It is also not the case of the Respondents that they were 
appointed in accordance with the extant rules. No direction for regularization of their services was, therefore, 
could be issued." 


Thus, in view of the law laid down by Hon’ble Supreme Court and High Court, as discussed above let us 
examine whether the petitioner has completed continuous service of 240 days in a l2-month of academic year just 
preceding from the date of this termination, i.e., August 5th, 2008. The Perusal statement of payment to the petitioner 
goes to reveal that work man was engaged as casual labor for the year 2005 to 2008, intermittently. Further, statement 
of payment of July 2008 goes shows that the petitioner, A. Mallikarjuna Rao, was engaged in the month of July only 
for 4 days, for which he has been paid wage of %30/- per day. Further, as per statement of payment in the month of 
June 2008, petitioner, was engaged by the Respondent only for 6 days, and for which he was paid wages of %30/- per 
day. Similarly, in the month of May 2008, the petitioner was paid wages of %30 per day for !2 days. Similarly, in the 
month of April 2008, he has been engaged for |l days but petitioner was not engaged continuously for the whole 
month. Further, Petitioner was engaged in the months of March 2008, for only 4 days. In the month of February 2008 
Petitioner was engaged for 0 days January 2008 for [7 days. Similarly in the month of December 2007 for 8 days 
November 2007 for 4 days, October 2007 for 8 days, and September 2007 for 6 days and August 2007 for l2 days in 
July 2007 for 0 days and in the month of June 2007 for !3 days. Thus, from the statement of payment of Petition it 
goes to show that the workman, was engaged by the Respondent intermittently during the period from June 2007 to 
May 2008 and has been paid wages @ %30 per day. Thus, from the above, it manifests that the petitioner has not 
worked continuously for 240 days in the !2 months of a calendar year just preceding from date of his termination i.e 
05.07.2008. Therefore, Petitioner has not fulfilled the condition precedent as enshrined u/s 25F of the I.D Act, and 
not entitled for protection of the provisions contained against retrenchment there in. 


Thus, the allegation of the petitioner that Respondent has terminated his services in violation of the provision 
contained under Section 25(f) of the ID Act is not established and proved from the evidence on record. Therefore, 
Petitioner is not entitled to for any compensation or notice pay or notice as per provision of section 25F of the Act. 
Thus, the action of the Respondent management in terminating his services is not illegal. This issue is decided against 
the petitioner, the workman. 


Issue No. IV: - 


In view of the finding given in issue No. II, the petitioner failed to prove that he was terminated in violation of the 
provision contained under Section 25F of the ID Act. Therefore, Petitioner is not entitled to any relief, and the 
Petition is liable to be dismissed.

The action of the Respondent in terminating the services of the petitioner, vide order dated 05.07.2008 is held legal 
and justified. Petition is devoid of merit hence stands dismissed. The Reference is answered.

Findings 
Issue No. I:- 


In this context, Respondent has submitted that the Respondent, Export Inspection Agency, Chennai, is a 
regulatory authority operating under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry in the field of pre-shipment inspection of 
notified commodities, and it was set up by the Central Government under Section 7 of the export, (quality control, and 
inspection Act, 963). Therefore, the claim statement is not maintainable as the Respondent is a Central Government 
department, statutory body under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, Government of India. And as such, this 
Tribunal has no jurisdiction to try this dispute under the provision of Industrial Dispute Act, |947. 


In this context, Petitioner, in his chief statement affidavit, has nowhere stated that contention of Respondent 
in the counter, that the Export Inspection Agency of Chennai is a regulatory and operational body and it does not 
come under the definition of "industry" as provided under the Industrial Disputes Act, is incorrect: undisputedly 
Respondent is governed by the Export, (quality control, inspection Act, !963). And its main function is to ensure 
quality control and pre-shipment inspection for goods being exported from India. Therefore, the Respondent Export 
Inspection Council of India, is not covered under the definition of "industry" as provided under Section 2F of the 
Industrial Disputes Act. The Export Inspection Counsel operate under the separate Act which focus on advising the 
Government and ensure quality control for Export rather than its activities like inspection and certification are part of 
its function as a statutory body for trade not industrial activity as defined under I.D Act 947. industrial Disputes 
Therefore, the claim statement of the petitioner raising industrial dispute is not maintainable under the provision of 
Industrial Dispute Act. This issue is decided against the petitioner. 


Issue No.II:- 


In this context, petitioner, in his claim statement, has alleged that he joined the service of the Respondent on

reports without assigning any reason in gross violation of the provision contained under Section 25F and 25N of the 
Industrial Disputes Act. Petitioner has alleged that has termination order dated 5" July 2008 is illegal, liable to be set 
aside. In support of his allegation. Petitioner has filed the affidavit of chief statement of his witness ४४५७], Therein 
१४५४] has reiterated the averment made in the claim statement. Whereas, in the cross-examination, WW has stated 
that the Assistant director of Respondent management, Shri G. V. George, engaged him on duty and there was no 
notification published for the post in any newspaper. Further, WW] states that on the verbal request of the Assistant 
director, he joined the work. Further, witness states that there was no designation for his post and no appointment 
letter was issued to him. His name was not sponsored through the employment exchange. Further, the १४५७४] 596 that 
he felt there was vacancy for four posts, and he may be absorbed in one post and he doesn't know whether the four 
vacant posts were sanctioned or not. Further, ४७४] witness states that he has made four representations to the 
Chennai head office and also to the office bearer of the Respondent office in the year 200I, and he was paid Rs. 20/- 
per day and later on he was paid Rs. 25/-, and Rs. 35/-, and lastly, he was getting Rs. 70/- per day. Further, the 
witness states that it is not correct to say that the amount of Rs. 20/- was paid to him in the year 200! for hourly basis. 
The witness WWI admitted that it is not an industry, but it is a central government agency, and there was no 
manufacturing or production in the Respondent office. Thus, in view of the statement made by the witness WWI in 
his cross-examination, it manifest that the petitioner had worked as a daily wager in the Respondent office and he was 
not issued any appointment letter for the post. The document filed by the petitioner in support of his allegation do not 
support the averment of the petitioner that he was appointed in the Respondent office on Group D post. However, 
petitioner has not filed any appointment letter, Attendance Register or salary slip in support of his averment that he

was appointed by the Respondent. The document exhibit W3, is a letter dated 26" December 2008, issued by the

As regard the contention of the petitioner that he has been terminated by the Respondent from the 
employment in gross violation of section 25F and 25N of the ID Act, Before examining the legality of termination of 
Petitioner it would be apposite to mention the relevant provision contained under section 25F of the IT Act, as 
extracted hereunder:- 


25-F. Conditions precedent to retrenchment of workmen:-No workman employed in any industry who has been in 
continuous service for not less than one year under an employer shall be retrenched by that employer until- 


(a) the workman has been given one month's notice in writing indicating the reasons for retrenchment and the 
period of notice has expired, or the workman has been paid in lieu of such notice, wages for the period of the 
notice. 


[xxx] 


(b) the workman has been paid, at the time of retrenchment, compensation which shall be equivalent to fifteen days' 
average pay [for every completed year of continuous service] or any part thereof in excess of six months; and 


(c) notice in the prescribed manner is served on the appropriate Government [or such authority as may be specified by 
the appropriate Government by notification in the Official Gazette]. 


Further, in this context, Hon’ble Court has laid down principles in respect of continuous service under the 
provision contained under section 25F of the ID Act 947 and also burden of proof these decision of Hon’ble Court 
are being discussed as here under:- 


(a).Further, in Rajasthan State Ganganagar S. Mills Ltd. v. State of Rajasthan and Anr. (2004) Apex Court held: 


"It was the case of the workman that he had worked for more than 240 days in the year concerned. This 
claim was denied by the appellant. It was for the claimant to lead evidence to show that he had worked for 240 days in 
the year preceding the date of his termination. He has filed an affidavit. It is statement which is in his favor and that 
cannot be regarded as sufficient evidence for any Court or Tribunal to come to the conclusion that in fact the claimant 
had worked for 240 days in a year." 


(b).In the case of Mohan Lal v. Management, BEL , 498] SCC P. 225. Hon'ble Apex Court held:- 


“Before a workman can claim retrenchment, not being in consonance of Section 25 of the ID act he has to 
show that he has been in continuous service of not less than | year with the employer who had retrenched him from 
service The Hon'ble Apex Court in this case also laid down the principle of how to count 240 days of service within 
one year and held: "Clause (2)(a) provides for a fiction to treat a workman in continuous service for a period of one 
year despite the fact that he has not rendered uninterrupted service for a period of one year but he has rendered service 
period of 240 days during the period of 2 calendar months for counting backwards and just preceding the relevant 
date being the date of retrenchment. In other words, in order to invoke the fiction enacted in clause (2)(a) it is 
necessary to determine first the relevant date, ie the date of termination of service which is complained of as 
retrenchment. After that date is ascertained move backward to a period of !2 months just preceding the date of 
retrenchment and then ascertain whether within the period of !2 months, the workman has rendered service for a 
period of 240 days. If these three facts are affirmatively answered in favor of the workman pursuant to the deeming 
fiction enacted in clause (2)(a) it will have to be assumed that the workman is in continuous service for a period of 
one year and he will satisfy the eligibility qualification enacted in Section 25-77 


(c).In the case of GM., BSNL and others V. Mahesh Chand AIR 2008 SC (Supp) 328, wherein the Hon'ble Apex 
Court have held, 


"It was the case of the claimant that he had so worked but this claim was denied by the appellant. It was then 
for the claimant to lead evidence to show that he had in fact worked for 240 days in the year preceding his 
termination. Filing of an affidavit is only his own statement in his favour and that cannot be regarded as 
sufficient evidence forany Court or Tribunal to come to the conclusion that a workman had, in fact, worked for 240 
days in a year. No proof of receipt of salary or wages for 240 days or order or record of appointment or engagement 
for this period was produced by the workman. On this ground alone, the award is liable to be set aside." 


(d). In the case of Range Forest Officer Vs. S T Hadimani AIR 2002 SC page !47, wherein Hon'ble Apex Court 
have held, 


"the onus lies upon claimant to show that he had in fact worked for 240 days in a year-In absence of proof of 
receipt of salary or wages record of appointment, filing of an affidavit by workman is not sufficient evidence to prove 
that he had worked for 240 days in a year preceding his termination.”

(e). In the case of Essen Deinki Vs. Rajiv Kumar, AIR 2003 SC 38 the Hon'ble Apex Court have held, 


"It was for the employee concerned to prove that he has in fact completed 240 days in the last preceding 2 
months’ period." 


(f). In the case of Municipal Corporation, Faridabad Vs. Siri Niwas AIR 2004 SC 468], wherein Hon'ble Apex Court 
have held:- 


"that the burden of proof was on the workman to show that he had worked for 240 days in the preceding 2 
months prior to his alleged retrenchment u/s 25F of the Industrial Disputes Act, 947. It is also held that it is 
improbable that a person working in a Local Authority would not be in possession of any documentary evidence to 
support his claim before the tr. Apart from muster rolls he could have shown the terms and conditions of his offer of 
appointment and the remuneration received by him for working during the afore mentioned period. He even did not 
examine any other witness in support of his case." 


(g). In the case of Chief Engineer, Ranjit Sagar Dam and another Vs. Sham Lal, 2006 AIR SCW 3574, wherein the 
Hon'ble Apex Court have held: 


"Fact that workman had worked for 240 days or more in year immediately preceding termination Burden of proof 
Burden is on workman not on employer-delay of nine years in seeking-no relief could be given to claimant." 
Manager, RBI, Bangalore vs. S. Mani & Ors., on 4.3.2005 AIR 2005 Supreme Court 2!79 have held that, merely the 
completion of 240 days of continuous service by the workmen by itself does not give rise to claim of permanence in 
the employment Hon'ble Supreme Court have held that:- 


"It may not be out of place to mention that completion of 240 days of continuous service in a year may not 
by itself be a ground for directing an order of regularization. It is also not the case of the Respondents that they were 
appointed in accordance with the extant rules. No direction for regularization of their services was, therefore, 
could be issued." 


Thus, in view of the law laid down by Hon’ble Supreme Court and High Court, as discussed above let us 
examine whether the petitioner has completed continuous service of 240 days in a [2-नाणात of academic year just 
preceding from the date of this termination, i.e., August 5th, 2008. The Perusal statement of payment to the petitioner 
goes to reveal that work man was engaged as casual labor for the year 2005 to 2008, intermittently. Further, statement 
of payment of July 2008 goes shows that the petitioner, A.R.V.V. Hanuman, was engaged in the month of July only 
for 3 days, for which he has been paid wage of %70/- per day. Further, as per statement of payment in the month of 
June 2008, petitioner, was engaged by the Respondent only for 2 days, and for which he was paid wages of %70/- per 
day. Similarly, in the month of May 2008, the petitioner was paid wages of 70 per day for 8 days. Similarly, in the 
month of April 2008, he has been engaged for |8 days but petitioner was not engaged continuously for the whole 
month. Further, he has been engaged in the months of March 2008, for 2 days February 2008, for 47 days January 
2008, for 45 days December 2007, for 8 days November 2007for l4 days , October 2007 for I5 days , and 
September 2007 for 8 days and August 2007 for 3 days and July 2007 for l! days June 2007 for 3 days. Thus, 
aforementioned statement of payment goes to show that the workman, was engaged by the respondent intermittently 
the for the period from June 2007 to May 2008 and has been paid wages @ %70 per day. Thus, from the above, it 
manifests that the petitioner has not worked continuously for 240 days in a said 2 month of a calendar year just 
preceding the date of his termination i.e., 05.07.2008. Therefore, Petitioner has not fulfilled the condition precedent as 
enshrined u/s 25f of the ID Act, and not entitled for protection of the provision contained against retrenchment 
therein. 


Thus, the allegation of the petitioner that Respondent has been terminated him from services in violation of 
the provision contained under Section 25(f) of the ID Act is not established and proved from the evidence on record. 
Therefore, Petitioner is not entitled to for any compensation or notice pay or notice as per provision of section 25F of 
the Act. Thus, the action of the respondent management in terminating his services is not illegal. This issue is decided 
against the petitioner, the workman. 


Issue No. IV:- 


In view of the finding given in issue No. II, the petitioner failed to prove that he was terminated in violation of the 
provision contained under Section 25F of the ID Act. Therefore, Petitioner is not entitled to any relief, and the 
Petition is liable to be dismissed. 


AWARD 


The action of the Respondent in terminating the services of the petitioner, vide order dated 05.07.2008 is held legal 
and justified. Petition is devoid of merit hence stands dismissed. The Reference is answered.

Order 


In view of the above said facts the Management and Applicant has not appear before the Tribunal and Management 
not filed any written statement/ evidence in support of his case affidavit, in spite of several opportunities given to him 
and taking into consideration the law as laid by the Hon'ble High Court in the case of V. K. Raj Industries v. Labour 
Court (7) and others 7967 (29) FLR 94 as under: 


“It is well settled that if a party challenges the legality of an order, the burden lies upon him to prove illegality 
of the order and if no evidence is produced the party invoking jurisdiction of the Court must fail. Whenever a 
workman raises a dispute challenging the validity of the termination of service if is imperative for him to file 
written statement before the Industrial Court setting out grounds on which the order is challenged and he must 
also produce evidence to prove his case. If the workman fails to appear or to file written statement or produce 
evidence, the dispute referred by the State Government cannot be answered in favour of the workman and he 
would not be entitled to any relief.” 


In the case of M/s Uptron Powertronics Employees’ Union, Ghaziabad through its Secretary v. Presiding Officer, 
Labour Court (II), Ghaziabad and others 2008 (7768) FLR 7764 Hon’ble Allahabad High Court has held as under: 


“The law has been settled by the Apex Court in case of Shanker Chakravarti v. Britannia Biscuit Co. Ltd., V.K. 
Raj Industries v. Labour Court and Ors., Airtech Private Limited v. State of U.P. and Ors. /984 (49) FLR 38 
and Meritech India Ltd. v. State of U.P. and Ors. 7/996 FLR that in the absence of any evidence led by or on 
behalf of the workman the reference is bound to be answered by the court against the workman. In such a 
situation it is not necessary for the employers to lead any evidence at all. The obligation to lead evidence to 
establish an allegation made by a party is on the party making the allegation. The test would be, who would fail 
if no evidence is led.” 


And by the Hon’ble Allahabad High Court in the case of District Administrative Committee, U.P. P.A.C.C.S.C. 
Services v. Secretary-cum-G.M. District Co-operative Bank Ltd. 2070 (726) FLR 59; 


Since 2022 workman is not present in-spite of notice and no written statement has been filed by the management in 
support of his case, the claims liable to be dismissed. 


For the foregoing reasons, the case is dismissed and the workman is not entitled for any relief.
