MINISTRY OF PORTS, SHIPPING AND WATERWAYS 
NOTIFICATION 
New Delhi, the 16th September, 2026 


G.S.R. 817(E).— In exercise of the powers conferred by sub-section (1) and clause (r) of sub-section (2) of 
section 113 of the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 (24 of 2025) and in supersession of the Merchant Shipping (Medicines, 
Medical Stores and Appliances) Rules, 1994, and Merchant Shipping (Carriage of Medical Officers) Rules, 1961, 
except as respects things done or omitted to be done before such supersession, the Central Government hereby makes 
the following rules, namely: — 


Chapter I - Preliminary 


1. Short title and commencement. — (1) These rules may be called the Merchant Shipping (Medicines, Medical 
Stores, Appliances, and Medical Officers) Rules, 2026. 


(2) They shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette. 


2. Application. — (1) These rules shall apply to all Indian ships engaged in international voyage and coasting trade of 
two hundred gross tonnage or more. 


(2) The provisions of these rules shall not apply in respect of any ship or class of ships or shall apply subject to such 
conditions or modifications as may be specified by the Administration. 


3. Definitions. — (1) In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires, — 
(a) “Act” means the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 (24 of 2025); 

(b) “Annexure” means an Annexure appended to these rules; 

(c) “Administration” means the Director-General of Maritime Administration; 


(d) “Medical Guide” means the Ship Captain’s Medical Guide published by the International Maritime Organisation 
and the World Health Organisation and as revised from time to time; 


(e) “Medical Officer” means a person possessing a degree in Medicine and Surgery from any University recognised 
under the National Medical Commission Act 2019 (30 of 2019) and enrolled as a medical practitioner on the register 
of the Medical Commission of any State; 


(f) “Official Pharmacopoeia” means the Indian Pharmacopoeia, the National Formulary of India, the British 
Pharmacopoeia Code and the United States Pharmacopoeia; 


(g) “port health officer” in a port, includes the health officer or any officer appointed by the Ministry of Health and 
Family Welfare or any officer working on his behalf, to perform the functions of the Port Health Organisation; 


(2) Words and expressions used in these rules and not defined but defined in the Act shall have the meanings 
respectively assigned to them in that Act. 


CHAPTER II -Medicines, Medical Stores and Appliances 


4. Scale of medicines, medical stores and appliances. — (1) Every ship shall carry medicines, medical stores and 
appliances as specified in the Annexure I in accordance with the latest standards issued by the Ministry of Health and 
the Ministry of AYUSH including the following, namely: - 


(a) Medicines in Part I; 

(b) Medical stores and appliances in Part 11; 

(c) Publications in Part II; 

(d) Instruments in Part IV; 

(e) Bandages, dressings and allied items in Part V; 

(f) Disinfectants in Part VI; 

(g) List of kits in Part VII; and 

(h) List of registers, logs and charts (addition) in Part VIII. 


(2) Where the total number of persons on board exceeds forty, one additional set of each item listed in sub-column (b) 
of column (2) of Annexure I shall be carried for every additional forty persons or part thereof. 


[भाग 1---खण्ड 3(i)] भारत का राजपत्र : असाधारण 43 


(3) Where a ship is required to carry a Medical Officer, the scale of medicines and appliances referred to in sub-rule (2) 
shall be carried for ninety-nine persons and the Medical Officer shall determine the additional medicines to be carried, 
taking into account the duration of the voyage and the area of operation. 


(4) Every cargo ship on the coasting trade of India engaged in voyages exceeding forty-eight hours shall carry the scale 
of medicines and appliances specified in in column (3) of Annexure I. 


(5) Every cargo ship on coasting trade voyages of forty-eight hours or less shall carry the scale of medicines and 
appliances specified in column (4) of Annexure I 


(6) The medicines and appliances specified under column (5) of the Annexure I, shall be kept under the control of the 
master. 


(7) The case containing the medicines and appliances shall remain locked, and the key shall be kept in the custody of 
the person authorised by the master. 


(8) The medicines recommended to be dispensed only under the instructions of a qualified medical practitioner through 
radio medical advice shall be administered strictly in accordance with such instructions. 


5. Scale of medicines for certain ships. — (1) Every cargo ship carrying chemicals wholly or in part, or the residues 
of chemical cargoes in quantities exceeding those defined in Volume 1 of the IMDG Code, shall, in addition to the 
medicines and appliances as per Annexure I, carry the medicines specified in Annexure II. 


(2) The recommended quantities of medicines specified in the Annexure II shall be used for treating— 
(a) inhalation cases of— 


(1) a crew complement of about twenty-four persons in a major emergency (such as an explosion) for up to twenty- 
four hours; 


(1) a few persons in a minor emergency until the ship reaches the next port or receives external assistance; 
(b) ingestion or eye-contact cases of one or two persons until landed or cured; and 
(c) skin-contact cases (leakage or spillage) of four persons until landed or cured. 


(3) Where the total number of persons on board exceeds twenty-four but does not exceed fifty, the scale of medicines 
specified in Annexure II shall be increased by a factor of 1.5 and where the number exceeds fifty, the scale shall be 
increased by a factor of 2.0. 


Explanation. — For the purposes of this rule, the expression “IMDG Code” means the International Maritime Dangerous 
Goods Code as prescribed by the International Maritime Organisation and as revised from time to time. 


6. Standards. — (1) All medicines, medical stores and appliances shall conform to the specifications laid down in the 
current edition of the Official Pharmacopoeias applicable in each case. 


(2) All tablets and capsules shall, as far as possible, be packed in proper moisture-resistant packaging to avoid 
deterioration under humid conditions prevalent at sea. 


7. Labels. — (1) All labels on containers or wrappings shall be rendered moisture-resistant by using an effective label 
varnish or other suitable means. 


(2) Every label shall indicate— 
(a) the generic name of the preparation as given in the Official Pharmacopoeia, in English; 


(b) the prescribed dose (maximum and minimum), strength, batch number, date of manufacture, date of expiry 
and the name and address of the manufacturer; 


(c) in the case of articles meant for external use only, a label bearing the words “FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY.” 


8. Poisons and controlled substances. — (1) Every medicine classified as a “poison” or “controlled substance” shall 
be clearly marked in capital letters, either in red lettering or on a red background. 


(2) All such medicines shall be kept under lock and key in a separate cabinet or chest, and the key shall remain in the 
custody of the officer authorised by the master. 


9. Record book. — (1) The master shall maintain an up-to-date record of all medicines, medical stores and appliances 
available on board and the stock, records and the Medical Chest Certificate in original shall be made available for 
inspection by the port health officer at any time. 


(2) The medicines, medical stores and appliances on board, together with the records, shall be inspected at least once 
every twelve months by the port health officer or Administration, who shall thereafter issue a certificate in the format 
prescribed in Annexure III.

(3) Where a ship is not likely to return to an Indian port within twelve months of the last inspection, the master shall 
procure sufficient medicines and certify compliance with the rules and record the details in a separate register: 


Provided that if a vessel is engaged continuously in international trade without calling at an Indian port for a 
period exceeding twelve months, the master shall in addition to certifying compliance and maintaining the requisite 
register, ensure that medical stores are inspected and certified annually by a competent foreign port health authority or 
a recognised medical practitioner in accordance with the Medical Guide. 


(4) A separate register shall be maintained for poisons and controlled substances. 


10. Substitutes. — Where any particular medicine or medical store listed in Annexures I and II is not available, a 
substance having a closely similar pharmacological action, or a preparation complying with the standards of the Official 
Pharmacopoeia, may be substituted with the prior sanction of the port health officer or Administration. 


11. Publications. — The master shall be guided in the use of medicines, medical stores and appliances, by the latest 
edition of the publications listed in Part III of Annexure I. 


12. Special precautions in endemic or epidemic areas. — The master of every ship entering an endemic or epidemic 
area shall carry adequate preventive and curative medicines as advised by the port health officer or Administration. 


13. Disposal. —The bio-medical waste from ships shall be disposed of as per Annex V of the MARPOL Convention 
or the guidelines on Bio-medical Waste Management issued by the appropriate authority. 


14. Replacement of medicines. — (1) All medicines consumed during a voyage shall be replaced at the next port of 
call. 


(2) Only those medicines having a remaining shelf life of at least one year from the date of purchase shall be used for 
replacement. 


(3) Intact sterile disposable packages shall be replaced when considered unusable by the port health officer or upon the 
expiry date, whichever is earlier. 


(4) The medicines, medical stores and appliances shall be replaced as advised by the port health officer. 
15. Storage. — (1) All medicines shall be stored in proper lockers or medicine chests to prevent displacement at sea. 
(2) Each category of medicine such as injections, ointments, tablets, syrups shall be stored on separate shelves. 


(3) The medicines shall be properly labelled with generic name, dosage, strength, date of manufacture and date of expiry 
and be arranged to facilitate easy identification. 


(4) The principle of First-In, First-Out shall be strictly followed for storage of medicines. 


16. Reactions. — (1) Before administering any drug, the individual shall be verbally ascertained for known drug 
sensitivities and in case any adverse reaction occurs, administration of the medicine shall be stopped immediately. 


(2) Where any persons experience adverse effects from the same medicine, the entire batch shall be sealed and reported 
to the port health officer at the next port, along with samples and details of the reaction and remaining stock shall be 
used only if permitted by the port health officer. 


17. Carriage of additional persons or supernumeraries. — Where supernumeraries are carried on board, the 
concerned seafarer shall ensure that any special medicines required for such persons (including family members) are 
procured in consultation with qualified medical practitioner. 


18. Special precautions regarding infectious diseases. — (1) Where any infectious disease or any event posing public 
health risk is detected on board, the master shall— 


(a) take measures to provide appropriate treatment to the patient; 
(b) isolate the patient; 

(c) take measures to prevent the spread of infection; 

(d) monitor contacts for symptoms; 

(e) ensure sanitary disposal of excreta in cases such as cholera; and 


(f) report the case electronically to the port health officer of the next port of call immediately through electronic 
mail or, where necessary, through VHF communication, port control, or the vessel’s authorised agent. 


(2) If a case of human immunodeficiency virus or acquired immunodeficiency syndrome is detected,— 


(a) the patient shall be kept under close surveillance, particularly regarding behaviours involving bodily fluid 
exposure;

(b) all syringes and needles shall be discarded as per the guidelines of the National AIDS Control Organisation in 
the Ministry of Health and Family welfare; 


(c) persons handling the patient shall use proper personal protective equipment, including gloves, and ensure 
aseptic disposal of infected materials and body fluids; 


(d) the case shall be reported to the port health officer at the next port of call. 


19. Exemptions. — (1) The port health officer at any port may, by written order and subject to such conditions as he 
may impose and for reasons recorded in writing, exempt any ship from carrying any particular item required under these 
tules. 


(2) The exemption order shall be issued in the form set out in the Annexure IV. 
CHAPTER III- Carriage of Medical Officers 


20. Carriage of Medical Officer. — Every Indian ship carrying more than one hundred persons, including crew, shall 
carry on board, as part of its complement, a Medical Officer possessing the qualifications specified in rule 21. 


21. Qualifications of Medical Officer. — (1) Every Medical Officer referred to in rule 20 shall— 
(a) possess a degree in medicine and surgery from a recognised University; and 


(b) be duly enrolled as a medical practitioner on the register of the Medical Commission of a State or Union 
territory.
