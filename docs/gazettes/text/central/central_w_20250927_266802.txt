In exercise of powers conferred under clause (d) of sub-section (1) and sub —section (2A) of Section 10 of 
the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (14 of 1947) Government Of India through the Ministry of Labour, vide its Order 
No.L-20012/113/2011-GIR(CM-D) dated 06/02/2012 has been pleased to refer the following dispute between the 
employer i.e. management of Kusunda Area of M/s BCCL and their workman through Vice President, Janta Mazdoor 
Mazdoor Sangh Vihar Building, Dhanbad for adjudication by this Tribunal: 


SCHEDULE 


“Whether the action of the management of Kusunda Colliery of M/s. BCCL in not providing 
employment to Smt. Sakuna Devi, the dependant widow daughter of late Radhi Kamin under the 
provisions of NCWA is fair and justified? To what relief Smt. Sakuna Devi, the dependant widow 
daughter of late Radhi Kamin is entitled to?” 


2. The case of the workman as per statement of claim is that the workman Radhi Kamin was a permanent employee 
of Bastacolla Colliery designated as Wagon Loader. She died on 18.09.2005 while in service leaving behind only the 
claimant Sakuna Devi, her widow daughter. The husband of the claimant namely Ram Balak Bhuia had already died 
on 15.02.1996 and after his death the concerned claimant has been living with the ex-employee depending herself on 
her as dependant. After the death of the ex-employee, the said Sakuna Devi has made her claim for compassionate 
employment under N.C.W.A. on 28.11.2005 and after consideration of claim she was called in Medical Board on 
20.07.2007 at 9:30 A.M. vide letter No. BCCL/Dhan/Area 6/2007 dated 18.07.2007 and accordingly she appeared 
before the Board. But in spite of fulfilling all the requirement and formalities, the concerned claimant was not 
provided employment till today. She has approached and represented before the management several times for 
employment but the management kept mum over it and when the management did not consider the claim of the 
dependent, she took up the matter before the union. An industrial dispute was raised in the office of A.L.C. (C), 
Dhanbad over denial to provide employment to the dependent of the ex-employee under N.C.W.A. on 19.07.2009 and 
the dispute was thereafter converted into industrial dispute. Ultimately no settlement was arrived during conciliation 
proceeding and the failure report was submitted on 13.09.2011 by the Conciliation Officer to the Ministry of Labour, 


6258 THE GAZETTE OF INDIA :SEPTEMBER 27, 2025/ASVINA 5, 1947 [PART II—SEC. 3(ii)] 


Govt. of India which resulted in the present reference. The action of the management in not providing employment to 
the claimant is not justified and the claimant is entitled for compassionate employment under N.C.W.A. and at last it 
was prayed to pass an Award holding that the action of the management of Kusunda Area of M/s. BCCL in not 
providing employment to Sakuna Devi, the dependent widow daughter of Late Radhi Kamin under provision of 
N.C.W.A. is not fair and justified. 


3. On the other hand the case of the management as per written statement is that the deceased Radhi Kamin, Ex- 
Wagon Loader was an employee of Dhansar Colliery. From the Service Excerpt of the deceased employee it is 
evident that there were three daughters of the deceased Radhi Kamin and Baiju Bhuiya namely (i) Sakuna-14 years, 
(ii) Sarsatiya-12 years and (iii) Kumari Sakila- 6 years. The claim for the employment was preferred by Smt. Sakuna 
Devi, widow daughter. The three daughters were reportedly married. The claimant Sakuna Devi has claimed as 
widow daughter of Radhi Kamin. Being a married daughter she has failed to explain the details of her in-laws and 
also did not disclose all the property inherited by her. Late Ram Balak Bhuiya S/o Late Vishun Dhari Bhuiya has died 
on 15.02.1996 and the Regd. No. has been indicated as 03/2007 dated 01.06.2007 as per letter No. 830 dated 
15.04.2009 of the BDO, Dhanbad. It has further been submitted that a committee was constituted by the Chief 
General Manager, Kusunda Area vide Office Order No. A.5/DOPM/09/860 dated 07.07.2009 to find out the 
dependency of Smt. Sakuna Devi upon the concerned deceased employee and residing with Radhi Kamin during her 
life time. The claimant Smt. Sakuna Devi was also intimated about the above vide letter No. 871 dated 3/4.7.2009 to 
appear before the Chairman of the committee/Area Manager (Planning), Kusunda Area on 06.07.2009 at 11:00 A.M. 
in the above matter with a copy to other members and the GM (P&IR), BCCL, Koyla Bhawan. The PM (MP&R) vide 
his letter No. BCCL/PA-VI/3(13)/Ar. VI/09/11868 dated 18/20.07.2009 were intimated in the above matter that the 
competent authority had regretted the claim for employment being the claim of indirect dependent and was not 
residing with the mother as well as being a doubtful case and she was not fully dependent upon the earning of Late 
Radhi Kamin during her life time. The intimation was given to her. It has further been stated that a complaint was also 
received about Sakuna Devi on 04.12.2007. Accordingly to that she was residing with one Rajesh Chouhan etc. The 
claimant had sought information under RTI Act, 05 which was supplied to her vide letter No. 2205 dated 06.12.2007 
along with letter No. 2825 dated 05.12.2007. A writ was also filed by the claimant Smt. Sakuna Devi bearing WP(L) 
No. 98 of 2010 and counter affidavit was filed by the management on 13.04.2010. The outcome of the same has not 
been intimated. In view of the above it is evident that the action of the Management of Kusunda Area is quite in order 
in not providing employment to Smt. Sakuna Devi as she was not the direct dependent and she could not prove the 
same in the course of investigation and did not disclose the details asked for about her in laws and the property 
inherited from them etc. There is no merit in the claim for employment. 


By way of rejoinder it has been stated that the statement vide Para | of the W/S of the claimant is as per 
record and reference of the 1.1). The contents of Para 2, 4, 9 and 10 of the W/S of the claimant are matter of record. 
The contents of Para 3 of the W/S of the claimant is not correct. The claimant was married daughter and not 
dependent on the earning of the deceased workman during her life time and no documents and information asked for 
could be submitted by the claimant before the management. The contents of Para 5 is not correct as would be evident 
from the W/S of the Opposite Party. The contents of Paragraph 6 of the W/S are not correct and the same are denied. 
The employment could not be provided and the intimation was given with reason thereof. She had filed writ and the 
outcome of the same has not been indicated nor disclosed by her. The contents of Para 7 of the W/S is not correct and 
denied. The contents of Para 8 of the W/S is partly correct so far taking up the matter by the union. The contents of 
Para 11 are not correct. The action of the management in not providing her employment as she is not direct 
dependent and not resided with the deceased employee and could not prove herself as being wholly dependent on her 
earning, the management has rightly not provided her employment as per the provision of the NCWA correctly.

8. Heard both sides and perused the materials on record. 


9. The Ld. Representative of the workman has submitted that the claimant Sakuna Devi has placed her claim being 
the widow daughter and dependent on the earning of her late mother Radhi Kamin for employment in her place as she 
has died on 18.09.2005 while in service of BCCL working as wagon loader leaving behind only claimant Sakuna 
Devi. She was married but her husband Ram Balak Bhuia has already died on 15.02.1996 and after his death she had 
been residing with her mother fully dependent on her earning. Her application was considered and she was also called 
for medical examination before the Medical Board but later on her claim was illegally rejected and therefore she 
raised this Industrial Dispute and prayed that the action of the management of Kusunda Area of BCCL in not 
providing her employment under NCWA is unfair and unjustified. 


On the other hand the Ld. Counsel for the management has submitted that after death of the Ex- 
employee, Radhi Kamin, the claimant Sakuna Devi made her claim before the BCCL for employment but during 
investigation by the committee it was found that after the death of her husband Ram Balak Bhuiya she could not 
disclose the property inherited by her husband. She has also not been able to prove that she was residing with her 
mother and was fully dependent on her earning and therefore her case was found doubtful and rejected by the 
management. BDO Dhanbad is not competent authority to issue dependency certificate to Sakuna and at last it was 
prayed to reject the claim of the claimant. 


10. Now, as regards the oral evidence of the management, MW-1 Debashish Bagh, Asstt. Manager Personnel, 
BCCL, Kusunda Area, Dhanbad has stated in his examination in chief that this case is related to the employment of 
Sakuna Devi daughter of Late Radhi Kamin who was Wagon Loader in Dhansar Colliery. As per records Radhi 
Kamin has three daughters namely Sakuna, Saraswatiya and Sakila and all of them were married. He had forwarded 
the papers of employment of Sakuna to the Headquarter but the same had been rejected. The information of the same 
was communicated to her. A committee had been established for making enquiry regarding employment of Sakuna 
but she had not participated therein so they had sent the report to the headquarter as doubtful case. In his cross- 
examination he has admitted that Radhi Kamin had died in the year 2005. In the year 2012 he was posted in Kusunda 
Area. He has no knowledge that Sakuna had been sent to Medical Board where she was found fit for employment. 
She had also submitted a dependency certificate issued by BDO, Dhanbad. He has also admitted that there is a 
provision of employment of dependent in case of death of an employee as per NCWA. The husband of Sakuna had 
died in the year 1996. 


As regards the documentary evidence of the management, on perusal of Exhibit M-1 it transpires that 
this is a letter dated 18/20.7.2009 written by Sri A.K. Singh, PM(MP&R) to the CPM, Kusunda Area by which he has 
informed him that the claim pertaining to the employment to Smt. Sakuna Devi widow daughter of Late Radhi Kamin 
was placed before the Competent Authority and the same has been regretted being an indirect dependent and was not 
residing with mother as well as being a doubtful case as she was not fully dependent upon the earning of Late Radhi 
Kamin during her life time. 


Exhibit M-2 is the office order dated 01.07.2009 of the Chief General Manager, Kusunda Area by 
which a committee has been constituted consisting four members to find out the dependency of Smt. Sakuna Devi 
upon the earning of deceased employee and residing with Radhi Kamin during her life time and the report was also 
directed to be submitted within 3 days. 


Exhibit M-3 is letter dated 03/04.07.2009 of the office of the Chief General Manager, Kusunda Area 
written to Smt. Sakuna Devi by which she has been informed that her application of dependent employment was 
under process and she was advised to appear before the committee at Area Office in the chamber of R.K. Raman, 
Chairman of the Committee on 06.07.2009 at 11:00 AM to ascertain the facts and process the same. 


11. Now, as regards the oral evidence of the claimant, Smt. Sakuna Devi has stated in her affidavited 
examination in chief that her mother Late Radha Kamin was a permanent employee of Kusunda Colliery under 
Kusunda Area designated as Wagon Loader who died earlier on 18.09.2005 while in service leaving behind this 
witness as her daughter and legal heir. She has further stated that her husband Late Ram Balak Bhuiya has already 
died on 15.02.1996 and after his death she had been living with her mother and after her death she had made her claim 
for compassionate employment under NCWA on 28.11.2005 and after consideration of the same she was called by 
the Medical Board on 20.07.2007 at 9:30 A.M. vide letter dated 10.07.2007 and accordingly she appeared before the 
Board but in spite of fulfillment of all the requirements and formalities, she has not been provided employment. She 
had represented before the management several times for providing her employment but all went in vain. She is legal 
and bonafide dependent of the Ex-employee, entitled for compassionate employment under NCWA. The action the 
management in not providing her employment is not justified whereas she is entitled for the same. In her cross- 
examination she has stated that her mother died in the year 2005. As per Service Excerpts her age is 14 years. It is not 
a fact that as per the calculation as on today she was 54 years. It is also not a fact that she is not entitled to get job.

12. Now, as regards the documentary evidence of the claimant, Exhibit W-1 is the Service Excerpts of Radhi 
Kamin, Ex-Wagon Loader of Dhansar Colliery and on its perusal it transpires that she had three daughters whose 
names were given in the column of nominee (i) Kumari Sakuna-14 years, (1) Kumari Saraswatiya-12 years and (iii) 
Kumari Sakila- 6 years. 


Exhibit W-2 is letter dated 18.07.2007 sent by Senior Personnel Officer, Dhansar/Industry Colliery to 
Smt. Sakuna Devi for her appearance in Medical Board on 20.07.2007 at 9:30 A.M. for medical checkup along with 
Service Excerpts and Academic Certificates. 


Exhibit W-3 is letter dated 12.07.2007 of Dy. CMO (MB) to the Dy. C.P.M./Agent, Kusunda Area by 
which he has been requested to direct the claimant Smt. Sakuna Devi, Yogeshwar Dusadh and Ashok Kr. Rajbhar to 
appear before the Medical Board, Koyla Nagar Hospital on 20.07.2007 at 9:30 A.M. along with Educational 
Certificate/ Service Excerpt of Ex-employee and all documents in original. 


Exhibit W-4 is the same as Exhibit M-1 i.e. rejection of the employment claim of the claimant Sakuna 
Devi. On its perusal it transpires that this is a letter dated 18/20.7.2009 written by Sri A.K. Singh, PM(MP&R) to the 
CPM, Kusunda Area by which he has informed him that the claim pertaining to the employment to Smt. Sakuna Devi, 
widow daughter of Late Radhi Kamin was placed before the Competent Authority and the same has been regretted 
being an indirect dependent and was not residing with mother as well as being a doubtful case as she was not fully 
dependent upon the earning of Late Radhi Kamin during her life time. 


Exhibit W-5 is letter No. 495 dated 02.03.2007 of the Office of Block Development Officer, Dhanbad 
sent to the Project Officer, Dhansar Industry Colliery by Block Development Officer, Dhanbad regarding issuance of 
dependency certificate. It transpires that this has been written in pursuance of letter no. 832 dated 04.11.2006 of 
Project Officer, Dhansar Industry Colliery and the contents of this letter reveal that on the basis of the enquiry report 
of Sri Ram Vinay Singh, Dhanbad it is certified that Smt. Sakuna Devi W/o Late Ram Balak Bhuiya who was the 
daughter of mother Late Radhi Kamin and father Late Baiju Bhuiya, was the heir of her parents and dependent on 
their earnings. 


13. On perusal of the above oral as well as documentary evidence adduced from the side of the claimant, Smt. 
Sakuna Devi it is evident that she has been able to establish and prove that she was daughter of mother late Radhi 
Kamin and father late Baiju Bhuiya and she was married to one Ram Balak Bhuiya who died on 15.02.96 and after 
his death she started living with her mother Radhi Kamin being fully dependent on the earnings of her mother who 
was working as Wagon Loader in BCCL and died on 18.09.2005 which also finds corroboration from Exhibit W-5 
which certifies that she was dependent on the earnings of her parents and as per NCWA, the claimant being widow 
daughter and dependent on the earnings of her late mother workman Radhi Kamin, is fully entitled to get 
employment. 


14. On perusal of the evidence led from the side of the management though as per Exhibit M-3 it transpires that 
the claim of the claimant Sakuna Devi has been rejected on the ground that she was not fully dependent upon the 
earnings of Late Radhi Kamin but this is not sustainable because the NCWA-II itself does not use the words “fully 
dependent ” rather it uses the words “almost wholly dependent” and secondly, there is no justification with the 
management to not rely on Exhibit W-5 which has been issued from the Office of the BDO, Dhanbad regarding 
dependency certificate of the claimant and if the management has any doubt regarding the genuineness of this 
document, it was for him to verify that he did not exercise and illegally rejected the valid claim of the claimant. 
Thirdly, the management has also rejected the claim on the ground of the claimant being indirect dependent which is 
in utter violation of NCWA-II because as per NCWA-II, widowed daughter falls in the category of indirect dependent 
and is very well entitled to the employment interalia if direct dependent is not available and here in the instant case, 
the management very well knows that direct dependent like husband, unmarried daughter, son/legally adopted son is 
not available and lastly, the management has also failed to adduce any evidence on record which may prove that the 
claimant was not residing with her mother after the death of her husband. 


15. Thus on the basis of above oral and documentary evidences of both sides this Tribunal finds and holds that the 
action of the management of Kusunda Colliery of M/s.BCCL in not providing employment to Smt. Sakuna Devi, the 
dependent widow daughter of Late Radhi Kamin under the provisions of NCWA, is not fair and not justified and so, 
Smt. Sakuna Devi is entitled to get employment under the provisions of NCWA and accordingly the management is 
directed to provide the claimant, Smt. Sakuna Devi employment within 30 days from the date of notification of this 
Award.

Central Government vide Notification No.L-2201 1/13/2015-IR(CM-II) dated 31.08.2015, under sub-section 
(1) and sub-section (2A) of Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 (hereinafter called the Act), has referred

“Whether the action of the management of Food Corporation of India in taking the date of birth of 
workmen as shown in the seniority list of the Bajaj Committee Report is legal and justified? If not, what 
relief the workman is entitled and from which date?” 


1. Brief facts of the case as per claim statement filed on behalf of workers union are that the Food Corporation 
of India (hereinafter called as FCI) is a statutory corporation set up by the Government of India under Section 3 of 
Food Corporations Act, 1964. In order to carry out its business, FCI initially engaged the contract workers in its 
godowns/depots and then in due course of time, it departmentalized the contract workers or brought them under the 
Direct Payment System of No Work No Pay System, thereby bringing them under the direct employer/employee 
relationship with it. The job of such workers consists of loading, unloading, stacking, de-stacking, weighment, 
salvaging and standardization of food grains. The FCI also gets the aforesaid food handling operations carried out 
through the contract workers or through its own workers at railheads and railway sidings as well. Respondent 10.1 & 
2 manage and control the affairs of the FCI at regional level and district level respectively.

Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act, 1970, employment of contract labour in 22 depots of FCI in Haryana

committee contract labour system was abolished in 22 depots of FCI including FCI, Karnal complex. 


3. Subsequent to the abolition of contract labour system in 22 depots of FCI, Haryana, a bipartite settlement 
dated 12.04.1991 (Annexure W/4) was entered into between the FCI and workers union for the regularization of the

departmentalization in accordance with the requisite labour strength of FCI. The FCI headquarter then came out with 
a circular no.IRL/8(1)(91) dated 11.06.1991 (Annexure W/5) prescribing the methodology/modus operandi for the 
identification and determination of seniority for the purpose of departmentalization. A detailed procedure for the 
identification of the contract workers has been laid down in paragraph no.3.1 of the aforesaid circular dated 
11.06.1991. Paragraph 4.2 of the aforesaid circular provides for the determination of seniority as per the length of 
service of a worker with the contractor. It further provides that if documentary proof of the age of a worker is not 
available, a medical check up to determine his age should be carried out or alternatively the worker may be referred to 
the local Government of Municipal hospital for determination of his age, if no documentary proof of the same is 
available. 


4. In pursuance of the aforesaid circular dated 11.06.1991, various committees namely Dalela Committee-I,

High Court of Punjab & Haryana determined the inter se seniority of the works of FCI, Karnal vide order dated

any person. The evidence of medical report, thus, is not a conclusive evidence. Mr. Bajaj in his report has 


[भाग 1---खण्ड 3(ii)] भारत का राजपत्र : सितम्बर 27, 2025/आश्विन 5, 1947 6273 


noted date of birth of all the petitioners. They have not challenged said report rather said report has already 
been upheld by this Court. 


24. A perusal of aforesaid paras made it ample clear, specifically para 11 mentioned above, that R. P. Bajaj 
Committee had considered the date of birth of the workers and has given its findings accordingly. Now claimant 
union again raised this dispute before this Tribunal as the matter has already been decided by the Hon'ble High Court 


and the LPA No.2543/2024 titled as Dilip Kumar and others versus Food Corporation of India and others thereof 


was also dismissed, wherein in para no.4, it is held as under:- 


“4. The appellants are stated to be working as ‘Palledar’ (Handling Worker) through a Contractor. They had sought 
absorption in the department and a Committee headed by Sh. R.P. Bajaj, retired District and Sessions Judge had been 
constituted to determine the age and the seniority of the workers. The Bajaj Committee in its report dated 05.07.2010 
had determined the seniority of the workers wherein the age and date of birth had been specifically mentioned. The 
report of the Bajaj Committee was challenged by some of the workers by preferring CWP No.23481 of 2014 which 
was dismissed. The LPA No.218 of 2011 thereagainst was also dismissed. The Special Leave Petition thereagainst 
was also dismissed.” 


25. When findings of the Hon'ble High Court have come regarding age of the workman, this Tribunal cannot re- 
determine the date of birth of the workers specially on the basis of the medical opinion as mentioned in Jathu Rai 
case (supra), the evidence of medical report is not conclusive evidence. To support this view, reliance can be placed 
on Criminal Appeal Nos.1119-1120 of 2016 titled as Mukarrab etc. versus State of U. P. decided by the Hon'ble 
Supreme Court of India. 


26. It is added here that so far as this argument of the ld. counsel for the claimant union that workers were not 
party in the Jathu Rai Case (supra), the same is devoid of merit as the case was filed by some of the co-workers, 
whose date of birth was determined by the Bajaj Committee and it was upheld by the Hon'ble High Court. 


27. So far as this argument of the Id. counsel for the claimant union that due to findings given in LPA 
No.2543/2024 titled as Dilip Kumar and others versus Food Corporation of India and others, the findings in Jathu 
Rai_case (supra), cannot be relied upon. In this regard, it is pointed out that Hon'ble High Court in ELPA 
No.2543/2024 titled as Dilip Kumar _and_ others versus Food Corporation of India and others has upheld the 
decision of the Jathu Rai case (supra) and it does not lie in the mouth of the claimant union that findings of Jathu 
Rai case (supra) has become irrelevant. Rather in LPA No.2543/2024 titled as Dilip Kumar and others versus Food 


Corporation of India and others, the findings given in Jathu Rai case (supra) have been upheld and thus have been 
authenticated by the Hon'ble High Court. 


28. So far as standing order is concerned, the same is undoubtedly issued by the FCI. However, the standing 
order is not having force, specifically when the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Mukarrab etc. versus State of U. P. 
(supra), has held that medical evidence is not conclusive evidence. Keeping in view the fact that medical opinion is 
not conclusive evidence. No reliance can be placed on the plea of the claimant union that on the basis of the medical 
evidence, their age should be determined. It is also pointed out that Hon'ble High Court in Jathu Rai case (supra) has 
categorically held that Mr. Bajaj in his report has noted date of birth of petitioners and thereafter, on the basis of age 
and experience, has determined seniority. 


29. So far as case titled as M/s Agra Electricity Supply Co. Ltd. v/s Workman (supra) is concerned, the same is 


not relevant for decision of this case. 


30. The claim being devoid of merit is dismissed. The reference is answered accordingly.

Central Government vide Notification No.L-22011/14/2015-IR(CM-II) dated 31.08.2015, under sub-section

the following Industrial dispute for adjudication to this Tribunal:- 


“Whether the action of the management of Food Corporation of India taking the date of birth of 
workman Shri Janki Shahni as shown in the seniority list of the Bajaj Committee Report is legal and

1. Brief facts of the case as per claim statement filed on behalf of workman are that the claimant was appointed 
by the management of Food Corporation of India (hereinafter called as FCT) on 03.05.1994 for working in FCI Karnal 
as A/labour. He had been working upto 30.04.2014 with the respondent no.1 continuously as A/labour and the work 
and conduct of the claimant remained good and satisfactory during the course of his service with the respondent 10.1. 
The services of the claimant were dispensed with on 30.04.2014 without following the procedure laid down in the Act 
as well as in the model standing order. The claimant has completed more than 240 days continuously as required 
under the Act, so he was entitled to the protection under Sections 25F, 25G and 25H of the Act, which the 
respondents has not followed. At the time of joining, the respondent got conducted medical examination of the 
workman and as per the medical certificate, age of the claimant was 34 years but the respondents had treated his age 
as 39 years and the respondents wrongly incorporated the date of birth of the claimant as 21.09.1955, which was 
mentioned in all service record i.e. appointment letter, CFP Slip, Pay Slip, Declaration Letter, Pan Card and Identity 
Card etc. 


2: The respondents have terminated the services of the claimant without considering the actual age of the 
workman. As per the date of birth shown in the service record, the claimant got the retirement on 30.09.2015 but the 
respondents has terminated the services on 30.04.2014, which is immature and against the principle of natural justice 
and against the provisions of the Act. 


3. The standing orders for the Industrial Establishments of the Food Corporation of India applicable to the 
departmental workers of FCI vide clause 3(5) provides that proof of the date of birth of a workman is adducible from 
the relevant documents like matriculation certificate, school leaving certificate or the register maintained by the 
municipality in which date of birth is recorded, the register maintained by the registrar of births etc. and in the 
absence of such documents the medical certificate given by the Govt. Medical Officer is to be relied upon for the 
purpose of the age proof. The date of birth of a person cannot be made liable to be changed to the caprice of the 
respondent/management. Clause 4 of the standing order provides for age of retirement as 60 years, but the 
respondents has totally ignored the said standing order and made premature termination of the claimant.

10. The conceded position emerging from record is that the petitioners were initially working with FCI through

their age should be determined on the basis of medical report instead of report of Bajaj Committee. The 


medical report indicates age of petitioners with margin of 4/5 years. It is a settled proposition of law that 


medical report especially ossification test is a corroborative evidence and it does not conclusively prove age of

any person. The evidence of medical report, thus, is not a conclusive evidence. Mr. Bajaj in his report has

mouth of the claimant that findings of Jathu Rai case (supra) has become irrelevant. 


27. So far as standing order is concerned, the same is undoubtedly issued by the FCI. However, the standing 
order is not having force, specifically when the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Criminal Appeal Nos.1119-1120 of 2016 
titled as Mukarrab etc. versus State of U.P. has held that medical evidence is not conclusive evidence. Keeping in 
view the fact that medical opinion is not conclusive evidence. No reliance can be placed on the plea of the claimant 
that on the basis of the medical evidence, their age should be determined. It is also pointed out that Hon'ble High 
Court in Jathu Rai case (supra) has categorically held that Mr. Bajaj in his report has noted date of birth of claimant 
and thereafter, on the basis of age and experience, has determined seniority.

30.04.2014 on attaining the age of superannuation of 60 years. There was no necessity of compliance of Section 25F 
of the Act, so there was no violation of any of the provisions of the Act. The claim being devoid of merit is dismissed. 
The reference is answered accordingly.

1 On receiving Order No. L-22012/4/2019-IR(CM-ID) dated 13/02/2019 from the Government of India 
Ministry of Labour, New Delhi for adjudication of the dispute, a Reference case No. 14 of 2019 was registered on

2. Koyala Mazdoor Congress filed written statement on behalf of the aggrieved workman on 11/11/2022. 
Management contested the Industrial Dispute by filing their written statement on 01/02/2023. Fact of the union’s case 
in brief is that Prakash Chandra Panda was posted at Patmohona Colliery and was earlier regularized from PR UG

Prakash Chandra Panda was regularized to the post of Looseman, Cat-IV from the post of Underground Trammer, 
Cat-HI instead of granting promotional benefit. According to the union, the stand taken by the management is totally 
wrong and illegal as the aggrieved workman has only been regularized from Cat-III to Cat-IV. He is entitled to get 
promotional benefit with one increment in his basic pay. Union prayed for providing direction upon the management 
of Patmohona Colliery, Sodepur Area to consider the case of Prakash Chandra Panda and grant him fitment of basic 
wages with pay protection from UG Loader to Timber Mazdoor and also give promotional benefit from UG Trammer 
Cat-III to Looseman, Cat-IV. 


3. Management of ECL in written statement has claimed that the dispute raised on behalf of Prakash Chandra 
Panda is misconceived and the Industrial Dispute referred by the appropriate government is not maintainable. It is the 
case of the management that the workman was converted from ‘Piece Rated’ UG Loader to the post of Timber

5. Mayuri Kar Verma, Asst. Manager (P) at Patmohona Colliery, Sodepur Area, ECL has deposed as

9. Having considered rival contentions and arguments advanced, it appears to me that Prakash Chandra Panda

higher category cannot be termed as regularization. In my considered opinion the concerned workman appearing

of the company is directed to pay arrears of incremental benefits which accrued to Prakash Chandra Panda from 
01/11/2011. I further hold that the management of ECL acted in an arbitrary and illegal manner by not granting 
promotional benefits to the incumbent and by fixing pay without change of any basic by treating such change of 
designation as regularization instead of promotion.

8. It is settled law that initial burden of proof to establish retrenchment in violation of provision of Section 
25 F of ID Act lies on petitioner. Petitioner has to prove by evidence that he has been in service of Respondent for 
240 days continuously in a calendar year just preceding from the date of his termination. 


Before appreciation of evidence on record, it would be apposite to have a glance of the relevant provision contained 
under ID Act and same is reproduced hereunder: - 


Section 25B defines the term continuous service which provides 
Definition of continuous service.- For the purposes of this Chapter, -- 


(1) a workman shall be said to be in continuous service for a period if he is, for that period, in uninterrupted 
service, including service which may be interrupted on account of sickness or authorized leave or an accident 
or a Strike which is not illegal, or a lock- out or a cessation of work which is not due to any fault on the part of 
the workman; 

(2) where a workman is not in continuous service within the meaning of clause (1) for a period of one year or 
six months, he shall be deemed to be in continuous service under an employer-- 

(a) for a period of one year, if the workman, during a period of twelve calendar months preceding the date with 
reference to which calculation is to be made, has actually worked under the employer for not less than-- 

(i) one hundred and ninety days in the case of a workman employed below ground in a mine; and 


(ii) two hundred and forty days, in any other case;

Section 25 F provides: - 
25F. Conditions precedent to retrenchment of workmen. 


- No workman employed in any industry who has been in continuous service for not less than one year under an 
employer shall be retrenched by that employer until- 


(a) the workman has been given one month 's notice in writing indicating the reasons for retrenchment and the 
period of notice has expired, or the workman has been paid in lieu of such notice, wages for the period of the 
notice. 


(b)the workman has been paid, at the time of retrenchment, compensation which shall be equivalent to fifteen 
days ‘average pay [for every completed year of continuous service] [ Substituted by Act 36 of 1964, Section 14, 
for " for every completed year of service" (w.e.f. 19.12.1964).] or any part thereof in excess of six months; and 


(c)notice in the prescribed manner is served on the appropriate Government [or such authority as may be 
specified by the appropriate Government by notification in the Official Gazette. ] 


Further, Hon'ble Supreme Court in its various decisions has explained the concept of continuous service in a 
calendar year u/s 25F of I.D Act. some of the relevant decisions are discussed here under:- 


In the case of Sur Enamel and Stamping works Private Limited Vs. their workmen, referring to Section 
25 8, Hon'ble Supreme Court has defined the term continuous service as under: - 


"The position therefore is that during a period of employment for less than 11 calendar months these two 
persons worked for more than 240 days. In our opinion that would not satisfy the requirement of section 25B. 
Before a workman can be considered to have completed one year of continuous service in an industry it must be 
shown first that he was employed for a period of not less than 12 calendar months and, next that during those 
12 calendar months had worked for not less than 240 days. Where, as in the present case, the workmen have not 
at all been employed for a period of 12 calendar months it becomes unnecessary to examine whether the actual 
days of work numbered 240 days or more. For, in any case, the requirements of section 25B would not be 
satisfied by the mere fact of the number of working days being not less than 240 days." 


In Mohan Lal Vs. Management of M/s. Bharat Electronics, dated 21.4.1981AIR 1981 Supreme Court 1253, the 
Hon'ble Supreme Court have held, 


It was, however, urged that section 25F is not attracted in this case for an entirely different reason. Mr. 
Markendaya contended that before section 25F is invoked, the condition of eligibility for a workman to 
complain of invalid retrenchment must be satisfied. According to him unless the workman has put in continuous 
service for not less than one year his case would not be governed by section 25F. That is substantially correct 
because the relevant provision of section 25F provides as under: 


"25F. "No workman employed in any industry who has been in continuous service for not less than one year 
under an employer shall be retrenched by that employer until: - 


(a) the workman has been given one month's notice in writing indicating the reasons for retrenchment and the 
period of notice has expired, or the workman has been paid in lieu of such notice, wages for the period of the 
notice; 


Provided that no such notice shall be necessary if the retrenchment is under an agreement which specifies a 
date for the termination of service; 


(b) the workman has been paid, at the time of retrenchment, compensation which shall be equivalent of fifteen 
days' average pay (for every completed year of continuous service) or any part thereof in excess of six months; 
and 


(c) notice in the prescribed manner is served on the appropriate Government (or such authority as may be 
specified by the appropriate government by notification in the Official Gazette)." 


Reverting to the facts of this case, admittedly the appellant was employed and was on duty from December 8, 
1973 to October 19, 1974 when his service was terminated. The relevant date will be the date of termination of 
service, t.e. October 19, 1974 Commencing from that date and counting backwards, admittedly he had rendered 
service for a period of 240 days within a period of 12 months and, indisputably, therefore, his case falls within 
section 25B(2) (a) and he shall be deemed to be in continuous service for a period of one year for the purpose 
of Chapter VA.” 


In Rajasthan State Ganganagar S. Mills Ltd. v. State of Rajasthan and Anr. (2004) Apex Court held: 


"It was the case of the workman that he had worked for more than 240 days in the year concerned. This claim was 
denied by the appellant. It was for the claimant to lead evidence to show that he had worked for 240 days in the year 
preceding the date of his termination. He has filed an affidavit. It is statement which is in his favor and that cannot be

regarded as sufficient evidence for any Court or Tribunal to come to the conclusion that in fact the claimant had 
worked for 240 days in a year These aspects were highlighted in Range Forest Officer v. S.T. Hadimani (2002 (3) 
SCC 25. No proof of receipt of salary or wages for 240 days or order or record in that regard was produced. Mere 
non-production of the muster roll for a particular period was not sufficient for the Labour Court held that the 
workman had worked for 240 days as claimed." 


In Municipal Corporation, Faridabad v. Siri Niwas (2004 (8) SCC 195), held "the burden was on the workman to 
show that he was working for more than 240 days in the preceding one year prior to his alleged retrenchment." In 
MP. Electricity Board v. Hariram (2004 (8) SCC 246) the position was again reiterated in paragraph I1 as follows: 
"The above burden having not been discharged and the Labour Court having held so, in our opinion, the Industrial 
Court and the High Court erred in basing an order of reinstatement solely on an adverse inference drawn 
erroneously ..” 


In the case of Manager, RBI, Bangalore vs. S Mani (2005) SCC Page 100, the 3 Judges Bench of the Apex 
Court held that "the initial burden of proof was on the workman to show that he had completed 240 days of service." 


In the case of Mohan Lal vs. Management, BEL 1981 SCC P. 225, Hon'ble Apex Court held, "Before a 
workman can claim retrenchment, not being in consonance of Section 25 of the ID act. he has to show that he has 
been in continuous service of not less than I year with the employer who had retrenched him from service." 


Now, in view of the provision contained under Section 25B of I D Act and Law laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme 
Court as discussed above, we have to examine whether retrenchment of petitioner workman by Respondent vide 
oral order dated 10.12.2002 is in contravention of the provision contained under section 25F. As per provision 
contained under Section 25 B of the I.D. Act, 1947, the petitioner workman has to prove the fact of continuous 
service of 240 days in a calendar year just preceding from the date of termination i.e.,10.12.2002 by leading 
documentary as well as oral evidence. As claimed by Petitioner he was terminated by oral order on 10.12.2002. 
The record goes to reveals that Petitioner has not filed any document in evidence to establish the fact that he had 
rendered continuous service for a period of 240 days in the Respondent office in a calendar year just preceding from 
the date of his termination i.e.,10.12.2002., Petitioner has not filed any document of salary slips or wage payment 
slips or attendance register or appointment letter or certificate in order to prove that he has been engaged by 
Respondent for work during the period of 240 days just preceding for date of termination i.e. 10.12.2002. Thus, 
Petitioner failed to prove that he had worked 240 days continuously in a calendar year just preceding from the date 
of his termination. However, Respondent has averred in counter that identity card issued by the Asst. 
Manager(Depot) is useful to show at the gate point only for entry into depot. The corporation will issue identity card 
with emblem in the prescribed form duly signed by the Area Manager only, for the regular employees. In fact, the 
corporation introduced Direct Payment System to the casual labour in the year 1997, itself. Under this system, the 
registers were maintained. The said register did not disclose the name of the Petitioner as casual labour. Hence, this 
contention of the Petitioner is not supported by valid proof. However, Petitioner has not rebutted or contradicted 
contention of Respondent by producing any cogent evidence. However, said Identity card filed by the petitioner in 
evidence pertains to the year 1997-1999 and do not pertain to year 2002 when the petitioner was terminated. 
Therefore, the Identity card filed by Petitioner have no relevance to establish fact of continuous service as required u/s 
25-F of ID Act. 


10. Thus, ongoing documentary and oral evidence on record, I am of the view that Petitioner has utterly 
failed to establish that he had worked for 240 days continuously in a calendar year just preceding from the date of his 
termination i.e.,10.12.2002 . Therefore, provision of Sec.25F of I.D. Act, 1947 do not apply to his case. Thus, for 
the want of evidence, the claim of petitioner that he has completed 240 days of continuous service in a calendar year 
just preceding from the date of his termination i.e.,10.12.2002 is not tenable. Thus Petitioner failed to establish and 
prove that his retrenchment is illegal in contravention of provision u/s.25F of I.D. Act, 1947, and the claim of the 
Petitioner for reinstatement in the employment of Respondent u/s 250 and 25H is not tenable. 


Thus, Issue No.I is answered against the Petitioner. 


13. Issue No.II:- As per record, undisputedly, Petitioner has not filed any appointment letter of his employment in 
evidence. It is admitted fact that Respondent used to take the work from him as and when required. Thus, it is 
established that Petitioner had worked as causal labour in Respondent management. Respondent in his counter has

terminating his from services arise. There was no employer and employee relationship between the Petitioner and the 
Respondent. Respondent in counter has contended that management being instrumentality of the Government, bound

As regard claim of petitioner for regularization in the service, Hon'ble Supreme Court in its recent decision has laid 
down principles and guidelines for regularization of casual workman, In the case of ONGC Vs. Krishan Gopal 
2020(3) Scale 272,Hon'ble Supreme Court of India have laid down the principle regarding regularization of 
the casual workman and have held:- 


“23 The following propositions would emerge upon analyzing the above decisions: 


[भाग 1---खण्ड 3(ii)] भारत का राजपत्र : सितम्बर 27, 2025/आश्विन 5, 1947 6289 


(i) Wide as they are, the powers of the Labour Court and the Industrial Court cannot extend to a direction to 
order regularisation, where such a direction would in the context of public employment offend the provisions 
contained in Article 14 of the Constitution; 


(ii) The statutory power of the Labour Court or Industrial Court to grant relief to workmen including the status 
of permanency continues to exist in circumstances where the employer has indulged in an unfair labour 
practice by not filling up permanent posts even though such posts are available and by continuing to employ 
workmen as temporary or daily wage employees despite their performing the same work as regular workmen on 
lower wages; 


(iii) The power to create permanent or sanctioned posts lies outside the judicial domain and where no posts are 
available, a direction to grant regularisation would be impermissible merely on the basis of the number of years 
of service; 


(iv) Where an employer has regularised similarly situated workmen either in a scheme or otherwise, it would be 
open to workmen who have been deprived of the same benefit at par with the workmen who have been 
regularised to make a complaint before the Labour or Industrial Court, since the deprivation of the benefit 
would amount to a violation of Article 14; and 


(v) In order to constitute an unfair labour practice under Section 2(ra) read with Item 10 of the Vth Schedule of 
the ID Act, the employer should be engaging workmen as badlis, temporaries or casuals, and continuing them 
for years, with the object of depriving them of the benefits payable to permanent workmen.” 


But in the instant matter, Petitioner has failed to adduce any evidence that he was appointed by Respondent by 
following recruitment procedure or he was working against any sanctioned permanent post in the Respondent 
management. Petitioner failed to adduce any evidence on record that the Respondent was indulged in any unfair 
labour practice by not filling the permanent post even though such post was available and by calling to employ 
workman as temporary or daily wages employees despite performing the same work as regular workman on lower 
wages. Therefore, for the want of such plea and evidence, the claim of the Petitioner for regularization in the 
Respondent service is not acceptable. 


Further, in the case of Hari Nandan Prasad v Employer I/R to Management of Food Corporation of India20 
(“FCI”), Hon'ble Supreme Court of India have held: 


“34. On a harmonious reading of the two judgments discussed in detail above, we are of the opinion that when 
there are posts available, in the absence of any unfair labour practice the Labour Court would not give 
direction for regularisation only because a worker has continued as daily- wage worker/ad hoc/temporary 
worker for number of years. Further, if there are no posts available, such a direction for regularization would 
be impermissible. In the aforesaid circumstances giving of direction to regularise such a person, only on the 
basis of number of years put in by such a worker as daily-wager, etc. may amount to back door entry into the 
service which is an anathema to Article 14 of the Constitution. Further, such a direction would not be given 
when the worker concerned does not meet the eligibility requirement of the post in question as per the 
recruitment rules. However, wherever it is found that similarly situated workmen are regularised by the 
employer itself under some scheme or otherwise and the workmen in question who have approached the 
Industrial/Labour Court are on a par with them, direction of regularisation in such cases may be legally 
justified, otherwise, non-regularisation of the left-over workers itself would amount to invidious discrimination 
qua them in such cases and would be violative of Article 14 of the Constitution. Thus, the industrial adjudicator 
would be achieving the equality by upholding Article 14, rather than violating this constitutional provision.” 


Thus, in view of the fore gone discussion and law laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court as discussed above, the 
claim of the Petitioner for regularization is not tenable. 


Thus, Point No.II is decided against the Petitioner accordingly. 


14. Point No.l: In view of the finding given at Points No. I & II, the Petitioner is not entitled to any relief 
claimed and his petition is found to be baseless, devoid of merit and hence, liable to be dismissed. 


Therefore, Point No.III is decided accordingly.

AWARD 


Sri U. Sambasiva Rao who worked as casual labour (who will be referred to as the workman) has filed this 
petition under Sec. 2A(2) of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 against the Respondent s Food Corporation of India 
seeking for declaring the oral termination order dated 10.12.2002 issued by Respondent as illegal, arbitrary and to 
set aside the same consequently directing the Respondent s to reinstate the Petitioner into service duly granting all the 
consequential benefits such as continuity of service, back wages and all other attendant benefits etc., and such other 
reliefs as this court may deems fit. 


2. The averments made in the petition in brief are as follows: 


The Petitioner workman submits that he was engaged by the management as a Male Casual Labourer in the Food 
Corporation of India at Hanuman Junction on 06.03.1995, and worked as Office Boy, also performing additional 
duties as Storesman and Watchman under the control of the third Respondent . He was drawing a salary of Rs. 1500/- 
p.m., at the time his termination. He worked continuously without any break in service. As per the management’s 
procedure, he submitted applications dated 17.9.1995, 6.12.1998 and 19.9.1997 to the second and third Respondent s 
for issuance of Identification Cards, which were issued accordingly. The second Respondent, through proceedings 
No. FCI/BSC/Hanuman Junction/S&C/1990-2000 dated 21.08.1999, confirmed the workman’s employment and 
joining date as 06.03.1995. The workman submits that the nature of his duties was permanent and he completed more 
than 240 days of service each year. The third Respondent issued a Conduct Certificate confirming his service as Male 
Casual Labourer. The claimant submitted a representation dated 06.12.1997 to the third Respondent for 
regularization. Based on this, the Assistant Manager (Depot) addressed a letter dated 19.12.1997 to the second 
Respondent , stating that the claimant worked as Gardener, handled maintenance work, and recommended

Respondent s came to the Buffer Storage Complex, Hanuman Junction, and orally informed the claimant that his

pay in lieu thereof, or compensation was given, making the termination void ab initio and liable to be set aside. After

6. Point No.I:-As per claim statement, Petitioner alleges that he joined the service of the Respondent Food

7. In support of claim Petitioner, has filed chief statement affidavit of WW1 and WW1 in his affidavit has 
reiterated the averments made in the claim statement, WW1 states that his service has been terminated by oral order 
on 10.12.2002 by the Respondent in contravention of the provision of Section 25 F of the I.D. Act. Petitioner has also 
filed photocopies of eleven documents in evidence i.e. Ex.W1, W2, W3 and W5. These documents includes 
representations dated 17.3.1995, 19.9.1997, 16.12.1998 and 16.12.1997 to the Respondent management to issue

8. It is settled law that initial burden of proof to establish retrenchment in violation of provision of Section 
25 F of ID Act lies on petitioner. Petitioner has to prove by evidence that he has been in service of Respondent for 
240 days continuously in a calendar year just preceding from the date of his termination. 


Before appreciation of evidence on record of Petitioner, it would be aposite to have a glance on the relevant provision 
contained under ID Act and same is reproduced hereunder:- 


Section 25B defines the term continuous service which provides 
Definition of continuous service.- For the purposes of this Chapter,-- 


(1) a workman shall be said to be in continuous service for a period if he is, for that period, in uninterrupted 
service, including service which may be interrupted on account of sickness or authorized leave or an accident 
or a Strike which is not illegal, or a lock- out or a cessation of work which is not due to any fault on the part of 
the workman; 


(2) where a workman is not in continuous service within the meaning of clause (1) for a period of one year or 
six months, he shall be deemed to be in continuous service under an employer-- 


(a) for a period of one year, if the workman, during a period of twelve calendar months preceding the date with 
reference to which calculation is to be made, has actually worked under the employer for not less than-- 


(i) one hundred and ninety days in the case of a workman employed below ground in a mine; and 
(ii) two hundred and forty days, in any other case; 
Section 25 F provides: - 

25F. Conditions precedent to retrenchment of workmen. 


- No workman employed in any industry who has been in continuous service for not less than one year under an 
employer shall be retrenched by that employer until- 


(a) the workman has been given one month 's notice in writing indicating the reasons for retrenchment and the 
period of notice has expired, or the workman has been paid in lieu of such notice, wages for the period of the 
notice. 


(b)the workman has been paid, at the time of retrenchment, compensation which shall be equivalent to fifteen 
days ‘average pay [for every completed year of continuous service] [ Substituted by Act 36 of 1964, Section 14, 
for " for every completed year of service" (w.e.f. 19.12.1964).] or any part thereof in excess of six months; and 


6294 THE GAZETTE OF INDIA :SEPTEMBER 27, 2025/ASVINA 5, 1947 [PART II—SEC. 3(ii)] 


(c)notice in the prescribed manner is served on the appropriate Government [or such authority as may be 
specified by the appropriate Government by notification in the Official Gazette. ] 


Further, Hon'ble Supreme Court in its various decisions has explained the concept of continuous service in a calendar 
year u/s 25F of I.D Act. some of the relevant decisions are discussed here under:- 


In the case of Sur Enamel and Stamping works Private Limited Vs. their workmen, referring to Section 25 B, 
Hon'ble Supreme Court has defined the term continuous service as under:- 


"The position therefore is that during a period of employment for less than 11 calendar months these two 
persons worked for more than 240 days. In our opinion that would not satisfy the requirement of section 25B. 
Before a workman can be considered to have completed one year of continuous service in an industry it must be 
shown first that he was employed for a period of not less than 12 calendar months and, next that during those 
12 calendar months had worked for not less than 240 days. Where, as in the present case, the workmen have not 
at all been employed for a period of 12 calendar months it becomes unnecessary to examine whether the actual 
days of work numbered 240 days or more. For, in any case, the requirements of section 25B would not be 
satisfied by the mere fact of the number of working days being not less than 240 days." 


In Mohan Lal Vs. Management of M/s. Bharat Electronics, dated 21.4.1981AIR 1981 Supreme Court 1253, the 
Hon'ble Supreme Court have held, 


It was, however, urged that section 25F is not attracted in this case for an entirely different reason. Mr. 
Markendaya contended that before section 25F is invoked, the condition of eligibility for a workman to 
complain of invalid retrenchment must be satisfied. According to him unless the workman has put in continuous 
service for not less than one year his case would not be governed by section 25F. That is substantially correct 
because the relevant provision of section 25F provides as under: 


"25F. "No workman employed in any industry who has been in continuous service for not less than one year 
under an employer shall be retrenched by that employer until: - 


(a) the workman has been given one month's notice in writing indicating the reasons for retrenchment and the 
period of notice has expired, or the workman has been paid in lieu of such notice, wages for the period of the 
notice; 


Provided that no such notice shall be necessary if the retrenchment is under an agreement which specifies a 
date for the termination of service; 


(b) the workman has been paid, at the time of retrenchment, compensation which shall be equivalent of fifteen 
days' average pay (for every completed year of continuous service) or any part thereof in excess of six months; 
and 


(c) notice in the prescribed manner is served on the appropriate Government (or such authority as may be 
specified by the appropriate government by notification in the Official Gazette)." 


Reverting to the facts of this case, admittedly the appellant was employed and was on duty from December 8, 
1973 to October 19, 1974 when his service was terminated. The relevant date will be the date of termination of 
service, i.e. October 19, 1974 Commencing from that date and counting backwards, admittedly he had rendered 
service for a period of 240 days within a period of 12 months and, indisputably, therefore, his case falls within 
section 25B(2) (a) and he shall be deemed to be in continuous service for a period of one year for the purpose 


of Chapter VA.” 
In Rajasthan State Ganganagar S. Mills Ltd. v. State of Rajasthan and Anr. (2004) Apex Court held: 


"It was the case of the workman that he had worked for more than 240 days in the year concerned. This claim 
was denied by the appellant. It was for the claimant to lead evidence to show that he had worked for 240 days 
in the year preceding the date of his termination. He has filed an affidavit. It is statement which is in his favor 
and that cannot be regarded as sufficient evidence for any Court or Tribunal to come to the conclusion that in 
fact the claimant had worked for 240 days in a year These aspects were highlighted in Range Forest Officer v. 
S.T. Hadimani (2002 (3) SCC 25. No proof of receipt of salary or wages for 240 days or order or record in that 
regard was produced. Mere non-production of the muster roll for a particular period was not sufficient for the 
Labour Court held that the workman had worked for 240 days as claimed." 


In Municipal Corporation, Faridabad v. Siri Niwas (2004 (8) SCC 195), held "the burden was on the workman to 
show that he was working for more than 240 days in the preceding one year prior to his alleged retrenchment." In 
MP. Electricity Board v. Hariram (2004 (8) SCC 246) the position was again reiterated in paragraph I1 as follows: 
"The above burden having not been discharged and the Labour Court having held so, in our opinion, the Industrial 
Court and the High Court erred in basing an order of reinstatement solely on an adverse inference 
drawn erroneously ..” 


[भाग 1---खण्ड 3(ii)] भारत का राजपत्र : सितम्बर 27, 2025/आश्विन 5, 1947 6295 


In the case of Manager, RBI, Bangalore vs. S Mani (2005) SCC Page 100, the 3 Judges Bench of the Apex 
Court held that "the initial burden of proof was on the workman to show that he had completed 240 days of service." 


In the case of Mohan Lal vs. Management, BEL 1981 SCC P. 225, Hon'ble Apex Court held, "Before a 
workman can claim retrenchment, not being in consonance of Section 25 of the ID act. he has to show that he has 
been in continuous service of not less than I year with the employer who had retrenched him from service." 


Now, in view of the provision contained under Section 25B of I D Act and Law laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme 
Court as discussed above, we have to examine whether retrenchment of petitioner workman by Respondent vide 
oral order dated 10.12.2002 is in contravention of the provision contained under section 25F. As per provision 
contained under Section 25 B of the I.D. Act, 1947, the petitioner workman has to prove the fact of continuous 
service of 240 days in a calendar year just preceding from the date of termination i.e.,10.12.2002 by leading 
documentary as well as oral evidence. As claimed by Petitioner he was terminated by oral order on 10.12.2002. 
The record goes to reveals that Petitioner has not filed any document in evidence to establish the fact that he had 
rendered continuous service for a period of 240 days in the Respondent office in a calendar year just preceding from 
the date of his termination i.e.,10.12.2002., Petitioner has not filed any document of salary slips or wage payment 
slips or attendance register or appointment letter or certificate in order to prove that he has been engaged by 
Respondent for worked during the period of 240 days just preceding for date of termination i.e. 10.12.2002. Thus, 
Petitioner failed to prove that he had worked 240 days continuously in a calendar year just preceding from the date 
of his termination. However, Respondent has averred in counter that identity card issued by the Asst. 
Manager(Depot) is useful to show at the gate point only for entry into depot. The corporation will issue identity card 
with emblem in the prescribed form duly signed by the Area Manager only, for the regular employees. In fact, the 
corporation introduced Direct Payment System to the casual labour in the year 1997, itself. Under this system, the 
registers were maintained. The said registered did not disclose the name of the Petitioner as casual labour. Hence, 
this contention of the Petitioner is not supported by valid proof. However, Petitioner has not rebutted or contradicted 
this contention of Respondent by producing any cogent evidence. However said Identity card filed by the petitioner 
in evidence pertains to the year 1997-1999 and do not pertain to year 2002 when petitioner was terminated. 
Therefore, the Identity card have no relevance to establish fact of continuous service as required u/s 25-F of ID Act. 


10. Thus, ongoing documentary and oral evidence on record, I am of the view that Petitioner has utterly 
failed to establish that he had worked for 240 days continuously in a calendar year just preceding from the date of his 
termination i.e.,10.12.2002 . Therefore, provision of Sec.25F of I.D. Act, 1947 do not apply to his case. Thus, for 
the want of evidence, the claime of petitioner that he has completed 240 days of continuous service in a calendar year 
just preceding from the date of his termination 1.e.,10.12.2002 is not tenable. Thus Petitioner failed to establish and 
prove his retrenchment is illegal in contravention of provision u/s.25F of ID. Act, 1947, and the claim of the 
Petitioner for reinstatement in the employment of Respondent u/s 25G and 25H is not tenable. 


Thus, Issue No.I is answered against the Petitioner. 


13. Issue No.II:-As per record, undisputedly, Petitioner has not filed any appointment letter of employment in 
evidence. It is admitted fact that Respondent used to take the work from him as and when required. Thus, it is 
established that Petitioner had worked as causal labour in Respondent management. Respondent in his counter has 
submitted that the Petitioner was never engaged as casual labour at any point of time, hence, no question of 
terminating his from services arise. There was no employer and employee relationship between the Petitioner and the 
Respondent. Respondent in counter has contended that management being instrumentality of the Government, bound 
by rules and regulations for recruitment of employees for the organization. 


As claim of petitioner for regularization in the service, Hon'ble Supreme Court in its recent decision has laid down 
principles and guidelines for regularization of casual workman, In the case of ONGC Vs. Krishan Gopal 2020(3) 
Scale 272,Hon'ble Supreme Court of India have laid down the principle regarding regularization of the casual 
workman and have held:- 


“23 The following propositions would emerge upon analyzing the above decisions: 


(i) Wide as they are, the powers of the Labour Court and the Industrial Court cannot extend to a direction to 
order regularisation, where such a direction would in the context of public employment offend the provisions 
contained in Article 14 of the Constitution; 


(ii) The statutory power of the Labour Court or Industrial Court to grant relief to workmen including the status 
of permanency continues to exist in circumstances where the employer has indulged in an unfair labour 
practice by not filling up permanent posts even though such posts are available and by continuing to employ 
workmen as temporary or daily wage employees despite their performing the same work as regular workmen on 
lower wages; 


(iii) The power to create permanent or sanctioned posts lies outside the judicial domain and where no posts are 
available, a direction to grant regularisation would be impermissible merely on the basis of the number of years 
of service; 


6296 THE GAZETTE OF INDIA :SEPTEMBER 27, 2025/ASVINA 5, 1947 [PART II—SEC. 3(ii)] 


(iv) Where an employer has regularised similarly situated workmen either in a scheme or otherwise, it would be 
open to workmen who have been deprived of the same benefit at par with the workmen who have been 
regularised to make a complaint before the Labour or Industrial Court, since the deprivation of the benefit 
would amount to a violation of Article 14; and 


(v) In order to constitute an unfair labour practice under Section 2(ra) read with Item 10 of the Vth Schedule of 
the ID Act, the employer should be engaging workmen as badlis, temporaries or casuals, and continuing them 
for years, with the object of depriving them of the benefits payable to permanent workmen.” 


But in the instant matter, Petitioner has failed to adduce any evidence that he was appointed by Respondent by 
following recruitment procedure or he was working against any sanctioned permanent post in the Respondent 
management. Petitioner failed to adduce any evidence on record that the Respondent was indulged in any unfair 
labour practice by not filling the permanent post even though such post was available and by calling to employ 
workman as temporary or daily wages employees despite performing the same work as regular workman on lower 
wages. Therefore, for the want of such plea and evidence, the claim of the Petitioner for regularization in the 
Respondent service is not acceptable. 


Further, in the case of Hari Nandan Prasad v Employer I/R to Management of Food Corporation of India20 
(“FCI”), Hon'ble Supreme Court of India have held: 


“34. On a harmonious reading of the two judgments discussed in detail above, we are of the opinion that when 
there are posts available, in the absence of any unfair labour practice the Labour Court would not give 
direction for regularisation only because a worker has continued as daily- wage worker/ad hoc/temporary 
worker for number of years. Further, if there are no posts available, such a direction for regularization would 
be impermissible. In the aforesaid circumstances giving of direction to regularise such a person, only on the 
basis of number of years put in by such a worker as daily-wager, etc. may amount to back door entry into the 
service which is an anathema to Article 14 of the Constitution. Further, such a direction would not be given 
when the worker concerned does not meet the eligibility requirement of the post in question as per the 
recruitment rules. However, wherever it is found that similarly situated workmen are regularised by the 
employer itself under some scheme or otherwise and the workmen in question who have approached the 
Industrial/Labour Court are on a par with them, direction of regularisation in such cases may be legally 
justified, otherwise, non-regularisation of the left-over workers itself would amount to invidious discrimination 
qua them in such cases and would be violative of Article 14 of the Constitution. Thus, the industrial adjudicator 
would be achieving the equality by upholding Article 14, rather than violating this constitutional provision.” 


Thus, in view of the fore gone discussion and law laid down by the Hon’ble Apex Court as discussed above, the 
claim of the Petitioner for regularization is not tenable. 


Thus, Point No.II is decided against the Petitioner accordingly. 


14. Point No.III: In view of the finding given at Points No. I & II, the Petitioner is not entitled to any relief 
claimed and his petition is found to be baseless, devoid of merit and hence, liable to be dismissed. 


Therefore, Point No.III is decided accordingly.

The claim of the petitioner Shri U. Sambasiva Rao to set aside his termination order w.e.f 10-12-2002 by 
Respondent Management of Food Corporation of India, Hanuman Junction is dismissed. Petition sans merit stand 
dismissed.

AWARD 


The Government of India, Ministry of Labour by its order No. L-22012/35/2019 (R(CM-ID)) 
dated 23.04.2019 referred the following dispute under section 10(1)(d) of the I.D. Act, 1947 for adjudication to this 
Tribunal between the management of M/s Singareni Collieries Co. Ltd., and their workmen. The reference is, 


SCHEDULE 


“Whether the action of the General Manager, M/s Singareni Collieries Co. Ltd., Ramagundam-I Area 
Manrcherial District in terminating the services of Sri Nadipalli Umapathi Rao, Ex- General Mazdoor, SCCL, 
Ramagundam-I Area with Effect from 18-02-2005 vide order No. RG4/PER/IR/049/161 dated 17.02.2005 is justified 
or not? If not, to what relief the applicant is entitled for?” 


The reference is numbered in this Tribunal as I.D. No 1/2021 and notices were issued to the parties concerned. 


2. Petitioner absent on the date fixed for VDE None appeared on behalf of the petitioner, nor was there 
any request to proceed with the VDE. It appears that the petitioner is not interested in prosecuting the case. 
Accordingly, a ‘Nil Award’ is passed. 


Award is passed accordingly. Transmit.
