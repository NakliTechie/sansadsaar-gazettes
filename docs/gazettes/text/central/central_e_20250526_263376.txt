ORDER 


This is a reference under Section 4() of the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 
967 in respect of the Notification no. S.O. 5053(E) dated 25.]|.2024 issued under 
Section 3() of the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 967, declaring the United 
Liberation Front of Asom (ULFA) along with all it’s factions, wings and front 


organizations as unlawful for 5 years w.e.f. 27..2024. 


The Government of India through the Additional Secretary, Ministry of Home Affairs, 
has issued a Notification dated 25..2024, under Section 3(l) of the Unlawful 
Activities (Prevention) Act, 967 (37 of 967) (hereinafter referred to as the “Act’), 
which has been published in the Gazette of India on 25..2024, declaring the United 
Liberation Front of Asom (ULFA)along with all its factions, wings and front 
organisations as an unlawful association. By the said Notification dated 25..2024, 
the Government has in exercise of its power conferred by the proviso to sub-Section 
3 of Section 3 of the said Act directed that the Notification shall have immediate effect 
from 27..2024 for a period of 5 (five) years, subject to any order that may be made 
under Section 4 of the said Act. 


2. Section 4() of the said Act states that where any association has been declared 
unlawful by a Notification issued under sub-section (|) of section 3, the notification 
is to be referred to the Tribunal for the purpose of adjudicating whether or not there 
was sufficient cause for declaring the association unlawful. The other sub sections of 
section 4 provides that the notification should be issued to the association affected by 
the declaration made under Section ३(]) and after considering the cause shown by the 
association affected, in the manner specified under Section 9 and after calling for such 
further information from the Central Government or from any office-bearer or 
member of the association, it shall decide whether or not there is sufficient cause for 
declaring the association to be unlawful. Thus, this Tribunal which has been 


constituted under the Act had been pleased to issue notice on 28.0!.2025 to ULFA

5. ‘Association’, as per Section 2(l)(a) of the Act means any combination or body 
of individuals. As per Section 2()(0) of the Act, ‘unlawful activity’ in relation to an 
individual or association, means any action taken by such individual or association 
[whether by committing an act or by words, either spoken or written, or by signs or 
by visible representation or otherwise], - () which is intended, or supports any claim, 
to bring about, on any ground whatsoever, the cession of a part of the territory of India 
or the secession of a part of the territory of India from the Union, or which incites any 
individual or group of individuals to bring about such cession or secession; (ii) which 
disclaims, questions, disrupts or is intended to disrupt the sovereignty and territorial 
integrity of India; or (iii) which causes or is intended to cause disaffection against 
India. ‘Unlawful Association’ has been defined in Section 2()(p) of the Act and it 
means any association, - (i) which has for its object any unlawful activity, or which 
encourages or aids persons to undertake any unlawful activity, or of which the 
members undertake such activity; or (ii) which has for its object any activity which is 


punishable under Section I53A or Section 53B of the Indian Penal Code [45 of 


30 THE GAZETTE OF INDIA : EXTRAORDINARY [PART II—SEC. 3(ii)] 


860], or which encourages or aids person to undertake any such activity, or of which 
the members undertake any such activity; provided that nothing contained in sub- 


clause (॥) shall apply to the State of Jammu and Kashmir. 


52. It has been held by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Jamaat-E-Islami Hind vs. 
Union of India, reported in [7995] I SCC 428, that the definition of ‘unlawful 
activity’ and ‘unlawful association’, as provided in the Act, makes it evident that the 
determination of the question whether any association is, or has become, an unlawful 
association to justify a declaration under Section 3(l) thereof should be that ‘any 
action taken’, by such association constitutes an ‘unlawful activity’ which is the object 
of the association or the object is any activity punishable under Section 53A or 
Section 53B of the Indian Penal Code (IPC). A declaration as regards an association 
under Section 3(l) as an ‘unlawful association’ has to be based on an objective 
determination of the matter by the Central Government. Section 4 of the Act requires 
confirmation of the act of the Central Government by the Tribunal. The purpose of 
making the reference to the Tribunal under Section 4(/) is an adjudication by the 
Tribunal of the existence of sufficient cause for making the declaration. The nature of 
inquiry contemplated by the Tribunal under Section 4(3) requires it to weigh the 
material on which the Notification under Section 3() has been issued by the Central 
Government; the cause shown by the association which has been declared as an 
unlawful association, in reply to the notice issued to it; and to take into consideration 
such further information which it may call for; to decide the existence of sufficient 
cause for declaring the association to be unlawful. The entire procedure contemplates 
an objective determination made on the basis of materials placed before the Tribunal 
by the two sides and the inquiry is in the nature of adjudication of a lis between two 
parties, the outcome of which depends on the weight of the materials produced by 
them. Credibility of the materials should, ordinarily, be capable of objective 
assessment. The decision to be made by the Tribunal is whether or not there is 
sufficient cause for declaring the association unlawful. Such a determination requires 


the Tribunal to reach the conclusion that the materials to support the declaration 


[भाग त---खण्ड 3(ii)] भारत का राजपत्र : असाधारण 3] 


outweigh the materials against it and the additional weight to support the declaration 
is sufficient to sustain it. The test of greater probability appears to be the pragmatic 
test applicable in that context. However, in the present case, the inquiry is being done 
in the absence of ULFA, as they have not appeared or responded to the notices issued. 
Thus, the inquiry is being held on the basis of the materials provided by the State 


Government of Assam and the Central Government. 


53. 35 per the constitution of ULFA, one of the main objects of the organization is 
to achieve sovereignty of Assam by secession from India, by armed revolution. It is 
mentioned in Section 2(I)(i) of the UA(P) Act, |967 that ‘secession of a part of the 
territory of India from the Union’ includes the assertion of any claim to determine 
whether such part will remain a part of the territory of India. It is thus evident that 
such a claim for secession, as incorporated in the Constitution of the organisation, 
comes within the scope and ambit of ‘unlawful activity’, especially when the 
organisation has been found to have taken action towards it, by committing illegal 


acts, by words written, by signs and by visible representation. 


54. On perusing the evidence and in terms of the submissions made by the counsels 
for the Central and State Government, it is seen that eighty nine (89) cases have been 
registered against ULFA and two hundred thirty five (235) persons have been 
arrested. 2(two) civilians and three (3) ULFA cadres have died. Charge-sheets have 
been submitted in twenty one (2l) out of the eighty nine (89) cases, while Final 
Reports have been submitted in fourteen ([4) cases. The cases involve bomb blasts, 
criminal conspiracy, extortion, recovery of grenade and other arms. It also relates to 
recruitment to ULFA and the issuance of press releases in the social media in support 
of ULFA. There are cases of hoisting of ULFA flags, dacoity, kidnapping/recovery 
of bombs and exchange of fire etc. There are also cases of ambush on army personnel 
and apprehending persons planning to jon ULFA. The long and short of the evidence 
that has been adduced shows that ULFA along with all its factions, wings and front 


32 THE GAZETTE OF INDIA : EXTRAORDINARY [PART II—SEC. 3(ii)] 


organizations have initiated a violent campaign, with the object to secede the State of 


Assam from the Indian Union. 


55. 35 the materials produced by the Government before the Tribunal shows that 
the organisation is involved in various subversive acts and activities, throughout the 
state, the action of the outfit comes under the purview of Section 2(0)(ii) of the 
aforesaid Act. Such unlawful activities pose a serious threat and are prejudicial to the 
security and integrity of the Nation. In the context of this case, none has appeared on 
behalf of ULFA nor have they produced any material as evidence to counter the claim 
of the Government. Hence, the evidence of the Government remains intact and the 
test of greater probability favours the Government. This Tribunal is of the view that 
the Government has produced considerable material before it, to confirm the 
declaration of the Central Government that ULFA is an unlawful association. This 
Tribunal thus finds that the materials produced by the State Government and on the 
basis of which the Central Government declared ULFA to be on unlawful Association, 


is justified. 


56. In view of the above discussion and the reasons assigned therein, the Tribunal 
has decided the reference forwarded to it under sub-section [l] of Section 4 of the 
UA(P) Act, 967 vide Letter bearing no. 0/03/2024-NE.V dated 23.2.2024, 
with the confirmation that there was sufficient cause to declare the United Liberation 
Front of Asom (ULFA) along with all its factions, wings and front organisations, as 
an ‘unlawful association’, in terms of the Notification No. S.O. 5053(E) dated 
25..2024.
