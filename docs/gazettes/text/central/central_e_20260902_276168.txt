WHEREAS, the Nagarahole Tiger Reserve encompassing an area of 843.96 sq. km. is situated in Mysore 
district (Hunsur, Periyapatna and Heggadadevankote taluks) and Kodagu district (Ponnampete and Virajpet taluks) 
in the State of Karnataka. It lies between latitudes 11°50'N and 12°25'N and longitudes 75°54'E and 76°18’E. The 
area was first constituted as Nagarahole Wildlife Sanctuary in the year 1955, covering an area of 285 sq. km. 
Subsequently, additional areas were included and final notification for an area of 643.39 sq. km. was issued in the 
year 1983 notifying the same as the Nagarahole National Park. In the year 2002, the National Park area was notified 
as an extension of the Bandipur Tiger Reserve landscape. 


AND WHEREAS, the Nagarahole Tiger Reserve was also notified as Core/Critical Tiger Habitat (CTH) on 
20" December, 2007. Further, an area of 562.41 sq. km. was notified as Buffer Zone of the Nagarahole Tiger Reserve 
on 14" August, 2012; 


AND WHEREAS, the Nagarahole Tiger Reserve forms part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve (~5500 sq. 
km.) and also forms an integral part of the Mysore Elephant Reserve. It supports very high density of Asian 
Elephants with significantly higher number of adult tuskers signifying its ecological importance; 


AND WHEREAS, the Nagarahole Tiger Reserve is contiguous with Bandipur Tiger Reserve and Wayanad 
Wildlife Sanctuary in the State of Kerala making it one of the largest wildlife habitat landscapes in the country. The 
Tiger Reserve is also linked to Bramhagiri Wildlife Sanctuary through reserve forests in Kerala. This entire 
landscape is also home to a healthy viable population of wild Tigers; 


AND WHEREAS, the Nagarahole Tiger Reserve also serves as a catchment of important perennial 
streams and tributaries of the Kaveri, Lakshmanathirtha and Kabini Rivers. The region is home to Jenu Kuruba, 
Betta Kuruba and Yerava forest dwelling Scheduled Tribes; 


AND WHEREAS, the region has very high floral and faunal diversity. The major forests of Nagarahole 
National Park and Tiger Reserve can broadly be classified into Southern Tropical Semi-evergreen forests, occuring 
in patches along the western boundary with Kerala, Southern Tropical Moist deciduous forests, which are found in 
the reserve forests of Hatghat, Nalkeri, Arikeri and south-western parts of Kakankote and western part of 
Metikuppe, and Southern Tropical Dry deciduous forests, which are confined to the reserve forests of Metikuppe, 
Veeranahosahalli, Kachuvanahalli, and parts of Kakanakote; 


AND WHEREAS, the vegetation of the region comprises of species of Shorea talura, Santalum album, 
Terminalia chebula, Anogeissus latifolia, Azadirachta indica, Chloroxylon swietenia, Acacia leucophloea, Acacia 
catechu, Stereospermum chelonoides, Zizyphus sps., Diospyros melnoxylon, Diospyros montana, etc. Lantana 
bushes cover large areas as under growth, including Phoenix acaulis at some places. Cassia tora, Cassia auriculata, 
Desmodium etc., also form the undergrowth. Acacia intsia is the common climber. Bamboos are generally absent. 
Sandal occurs profusely in this type of forests; 


AND WHEREAS, the diverse fauna of Nagarahole Tiger Reserve is due to its ecological variations from 
dry deciduous forests in the eastern regions of the National Park to tropical moist deciduous forests in the western 
parts of the National Park. The unique grassy swamps locally called ‘Hadlus’ provide forage for herbivores through 
the dry periods of summer. The elevation of the Tiger Reserve varies from 700 m to 960 m. The region has a 
tropical climate with rainfall ranging from 1000 mm. to 1500 mm.

paragraph 4 thereof, shall be scrutinized by the Monitoring Committee based on the actual site-specific 
conditions and referred to the Central Government in the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate 
Change for prior environmental clearances under the provisions of the said notification.

the erstwhile Ministry of Environment and Forests number 5.0. 1533(E), dated the 147 September, 2006 and 
are falling in the Eco-sensitive Zone, except for the prohibited activities as specified in the Table under 
paragraph 4 thereof, shall be scrutinized by the Monitoring Committee based on the actual site-specific 
conditions and referred to the concerned regulatory authorities. 

(5) The Member Secretary of Monitoring Committee shall be competent to file complaints under 
section 19 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 against any person who contravenes the provisions of 
this notification.

representatives from Industry Associations or concerned stakeholders to assist in its deliberations depending 
on the requirements on issue to issue basis.
