As per report of the stamp reporter, this election petition has been filed beyond time by 458 days. 


As per Section 8 of The Representation of People Act, 95 (hereinafter referred to as 'The Act, 95'), 
election petition has to be presented within 45 days. 


In Election Petition No.l of 20i4 (Mohan Lal And Another vs. State Of U.P. through Secretary to The Chairman 
And 7 Others) decided on 8.04.204 and in Election Petition No.2 of 202] (Ram Nath Priyadarshi Suman vs. 
The Chief Election Commissioner Of India And 3 Others) decided on 23.07.202, it has been held that an 
election petition presented beyond the period of limitation provided under Section 8l of The Act, l95], is a 
petition which does not comply with the statutory provisions of Section 8] of The Act, l95] and, consequently, it 
is liable to be dismissed. It has also been held that the provisions of the Limitation Act shall not apply to an 
election petition. 


The judgement in the Election Petition No. of 20I4 (Mohan Lal And Another vs. State Of U.P. through 
Secretary to The Chairman And 7 Others) decided on 8.04.204 is reproduced below: 


"The petition was adjourned on the last date. 
No one appears for the petitioners even today even though the name of the counsel is printed in the cause list. 
I have perused the petition. 


This is an election petition filed by two petitioners for quashing the order dated 6.2.203 declaring the 
election of Smt. Laxmi Gautam, respondent No.4 from the 3! Legislative Assembly (Reserve Constituency) 
Chandausi, U.P. 


The election petition was presented on 3.2.204. 


The petition as per the office report dated 23..20l4 is beyond time by | year 278 days. There is no 
objection against the said report. 


Section 8[ of the Act provides for presenting an election petition on the pecified grounds within forty five 
days of the election of the returned candidate or if there are more than one returned candidate and the dates 
of their election are different, the later of those two dates. 


The petitioners in paragraph 6 of the petition states the election result was declared on 6.3.20i2 and the 
respondent No.4 (wrongly mentioned as respondent No.5) was placed at serial No.! meaning to have been 
declared elected. 


The petition is therefore clearly beyond time as reported. 


An election petition is not an action in common law or in equity but under a statute which provides that no 
election to the Parliament or to any State Legislature can be called in question except by an election petition 
presented in accordance with the Representation of the People Act, !95l. Therefore, the rule of statutory 
limitation applies to election petitions. 


The election petition which is in the nature of original proceedings has to be presented strictly within the 
limitation contained in the Act. There is no provision in the Act to condone the delay in its filing. The Act is 
a complete and a self contained code which does not admit introduction of the principles or the provisions of 
law contained in the Limitation Act and, therefore, the limitation Act is not applicable to the election 
petitions vide K. Venkereswara Rao and another Vs. Bekkam Narsimha Reddi and others AIR 969 SC 
872. 


In Hukumdev Narain Yaday Vs. Lalit Narain Misra AIR 974 SC 480 it has been held that even if the 
special law i.e. Representation of Peoples Act, 95! does not specifically exclude the provisions of Section 4 
to 24 of the Limitation Act nonethelessas the Act is a complete code in itself the provisions of the Limitation 
Act must be held to be necessarily excluded. Therefore, as the Limitation Act is not applicable to the 
election petitions under the Act, the delay in presentation of election petition isnot liable to be condoned. 


338 THE GAZETTE OF INDIA : OCTOBER 44, 2023/ASVINA 22, 945 [PART II—SEC. 3(iii)] 


The Court otherwise also has no power to condone any delay in the filing of the election petition. 


Section 86(l) of the Representatives of Peoples Act, l95 provides that the High Court shall dismiss an 
election petition for non compliance of provisions of Section 8[ or Section 82 or Section 7 and the order 
dismissing the election petition under this sub-section shall be deemed to be an order made under clause (l) 
of Section 98 of the Act which is liable to be dismissed under Section 86() of the Act. 


In view of the above, as the election petition has been presented beyond the period of limitation provided 
under Section 8! of the Act, it is a petition which does not comply with the statutory provision of Section 8] 
of the Act. 


Accordingly, the petition is dismissed summarily under Section 86 of the Act for non compliance of 
provisions of Section 8] of the Act."
